A community college I worked with had a new registrar's assistant in her second week. She'd been through orientation, shadowed two supervisors, and started executing work under supervision. On day nine, she asked her supervisor why the residency-appeal workflow required three signatures when the state manual described a two-signature process.

Her supervisor had never noticed. Nobody had. The third signature had been added seven years earlier for a specific case that had since resolved. It had stayed in the workflow because nobody had thought to remove it.

The new staff member saw what everyone who had been there longer had absorbed into normal practice. Within thirty days, the view would fade. By ninety days, she'd stop noticing.

Institutions that capture the first-ninety-days view turn onboarding into documented improvement. Most institutions don't. The learning window closes before it produces anything the institution keeps.
