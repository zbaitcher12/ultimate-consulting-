Three numbers to put against the 24 months after go-live.

The modernization-value shortfall at institutions that didn't plan the 24-month post-go-live optimization window: $1M–$5M in unrealized system value over the 24 months — because staff worked around the system rather than through it, and the configurations that should have been adjusted stayed as-installed.

The post-modernization operational-cost drag at institutions where documentation wasn't refreshed against the new system inside 12 months of go-live: $300K–$900K annually in exception handling, manual bridges, and shadow tracking staff built to compensate for documentation still describing the retired system.

The audit-finding count differential at years two and three post-modernization between institutions that ran a planned optimization program versus institutions that drifted: 4 to 8 additional findings on the drifted side, sourced from workflows the new system was executing against an unrefreshed SOP set.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to structure a planned 24-month optimization program — with a named owner, quarterly milestones, and dedicated documentation refresh capacity: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Sustaining operating maturity post-modernization isn't automatic. It's a planned commitment. Modernization is the visible work. Optimization is what produces the value modernization was supposed to deliver. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
