Three numbers to put against your HR calendar.

The wrongful-termination judgment when the institutional record didn't support the decision because progressive-discipline documentation was inconsistent or missing: $150K–$600K per case, before legal defense costs that typically run six figures on top.

The corrective action cost from a Title IX finding when the response workflow wasn't on paper against the current federal timing requirements: $100K–$500K in review, remediation, and mandated procedural changes — plus the private litigation exposure that runs in parallel.

The I-9 exposure at institutions whose onboarding sequence didn't include documented verification routing: $2,300 to $27,000 per affected file under current ICE penalty schedules, in a workforce where onboarding turnover produces dozens of affected files per year.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the four HR workflows on paper at operational depth — including the exception routing and the timing requirements: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance requires written procedures across every federally-funded workflow, and HR workflows tied to federally-funded personnel fall inside that scope. The absence of a written procedure isn't neutral. In litigation, it's dispositive.

HR documentation is uncomfortable work. Absent documentation is more dispositive than imperfect documentation. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
