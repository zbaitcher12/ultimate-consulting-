Format: ~85 words. Direct engagement bait.

The HR SOPs that hold up in litigation are the ones that name the steps, the timing, the actors, the documentation requirements, and the escalation routing — before the trigger event lands.

The SOPs that fail are the policy statements written after the fact, or the practices that lived only in the HR director's supervisory judgment.

The gap becomes visible only under discovery. By then, the deferral cost is already priced.

Which HR SOP at your institution would hold up under discovery — and which one would you rather rewrite before you need it?
