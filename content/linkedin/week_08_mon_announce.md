A public regional university I worked with ran an experiment at the end of a cabinet meeting. The provost picked a cross-functional exception the institution had resolved the previous week — an aid-plus-bursar timing question that had escalated. He asked three staff members at three different levels who owned the decision.

He got three different answers.

Then he asked the person the RACI named as the owner. She hadn't been in the conversation. She'd found out the decision had been made when the president's chief of staff mentioned it in passing.

The documented chain and the chain that actually ran weren't just misaligned. They didn't share more than one name between them.

The exercise took an hour. The gap it surfaced had been running informally for four years. The cabinet had been absorbing the cost of reconstructing the chain from memory every time an exception hit.
