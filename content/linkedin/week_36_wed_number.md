Three numbers to put against your IT portfolio.

The cost of running IT operational documentation and cybersecurity control documentation as two separate portfolios: $200K–$500K annually in duplicative maintenance, version-reconciliation, and cross-audit response work — at a mid-size institution where the two portfolios could have been one.

The cyber-insurance premium impact when documented controls are incomplete or out-of-date at renewal: 20% to 60% premium increase, plus tightened coverage terms and higher retentions — before any breach occurs.

The GLBA Safeguards Rule finding when the written information-security program didn't align to the NIST 800-171 framework ED referenced in its Dec 2020 and Feb 2023 guidance: $75K–$400K in remediation, plus the administrative-capability determination that flows to FSA.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to unify the four IT workflows — change management, access provisioning, incident response, data handling — as one portfolio serving both purposes: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

GLBA Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314) requires a written information-security program with a qualified individual, written risk assessments, and service-provider oversight — mapped to NIST 800-171 for federal student-aid data. The same documents satisfy both the cyber audit and the single audit.

Capture once, comply twice. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"
