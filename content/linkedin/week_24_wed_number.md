Three numbers to put against the next brand refresh.

The cost of a full institutional brand refresh commissioned by a new CMO: $250K–$800K in outside fees, staff time, asset production, and the follow-on rollout across channels — repeated on average every four to six years as CMO tenure turns over.

The productivity loss during the six-month post-CMO-arrival brand-audit period: 30% to 50% of the marketing team's campaign capacity redirected to catalog work, audit response, and interim standards documentation.

The annual brand-erosion cost at institutions whose asset library governance was undocumented — meaning staff used stale logos, off-brand colors, or unapproved photography because current assets weren't findable: 15% to 30% of external content out-of-standard, which is a measurable trust and recruitment drag.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the three marketing workflows on paper — campaign brief, approval chain, asset library governance — at operational depth: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Institutional consistency isn't a brand-team concern. It's an enrollment concern, an advancement concern, and a board concern. The brand that resets every four years is a brand that never compounds.

Campaigns are the visible work. SOPs are what makes the brand survive the team. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
