A community college president I met with this summer said the same thing three of her peers had said the week before. The compliance letters were getting longer. The AI vendor pitches were getting more aggressive. The enrollment projections for the fall were softer than they'd been in a decade.

None of the three had budgeted for the record of how the work actually runs. All three had absorbed the carrying cost of operating in the dark for years without seeing it on a line item.

Then federal review cadence tightened. Then someone in the cabinet asked what would happen if the aid director retired. Then an AI pilot doubled the volume the bursar's exception queue was already absorbing.

The three pressures didn't announce themselves as one problem. They arrived at the same door within eighteen months. The institutions that had put the work on paper earlier absorbed the pressure. The ones that hadn't, felt it.
