Three numbers to put against the next document handoff.

The time-to-decay for documentation delivered without a named internal owner and a refresh cadence: 8 to 14 months from delivery to material drift from operational reality — at which point the document is either referenced as if current (producing inconsistency) or ignored (producing the original gap).

The sunk-cost hit on documentation work whose governance was deferred until after delivery: $60K–$180K per project, functionally written off because the finished document never became a load-bearing asset.

The lift in "still current and in use" rate at year three when governance was named at scoping: 4x to 6x higher than at institutions where governance was left for after — meaning the initial investment produces four to six times the multi-year return.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to name the governance model — owner, cadence, change-control workflow — before the work closes: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Ownership post-handoff is not a nice-to-have. It's the single decision that determines whether the work builds an institutional asset or produces a document that decays before its second birthday.

The institutions that decide before closure build portfolios. The institutions that decide later, or not at all, rebuild every two years. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
