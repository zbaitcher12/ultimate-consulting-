Three numbers to put against your IR calendar.

The reporting-capacity loss at institutions whose IR director departed without documented data definitions and refresh logic: 4 to 9 months of stalled dashboards, delayed IPEDS filings, and cabinet decisions made on numbers the successor couldn't fully reconcile.

The FERPA exposure at institutions whose data-request routing lived in one trained IR staff member: 15% to 30% of ad hoc data requests handled outside the documented consent boundary — every one of them a potential disclosure finding.

The NIST 800-171 gap at institutions whose data-classification framework existed in IR memory but wasn't on paper at the workflow level: 6 to 12 CUI-handling deficiencies surfaced on the next controls assessment, each requiring remediation before the next Title IV audit cycle.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the four IR gaps on paper — data definitions, refresh cadence, dashboard governance, FERPA boundaries — at the level a successor can execute from: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

NIST 800-171 requires documented data-classification and handling. It's not enough for the IR director to know which data is CUI and which isn't. The classification has to be in writing, with routing rules the successor can follow without asking.

Most IR functions are one staff member deep. The SOPs are what make that survivable. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
