A public regional university I worked with had run a successful pilot on one workflow. Narrow scope. Engaged sponsor. Committed participants. Clean landing. The team exited the pilot with a documented workflow, a named owner, and a refresh cadence.

Then the cabinet asked what came next. The institution had one workflow documented and ninety-nine that still needed attention.

The pilot model that worked for one doesn't obviously translate to ninety-nine. Three rollout patterns work, and each fits a different risk profile. Function-by-function — highest-risk function first, then the next. Risk-tier-by-risk-tier — the highest-risk workflows across the whole org chart, regardless of which function they sit in. Decision-authority-first — the cross-functional decisions before the within-function workflows.

The institution picked the wrong pattern the first time. Function-by-function, when the actual risk concentration was in the cross-functional handoffs. Six months in, the highest-risk workflows were still undocumented.
