Three numbers to put against your incident-response calendar.

The cost of an incident response failure at 2 a.m. when the on-call staff member couldn't find the runbook, contacted the wrong people in the wrong order, and preserved evidence inconsistently: $250K–$1.5M in extended incident duration, notification exposure, and forensic reconstruction — before the regulatory findings that follow.

The ransomware recovery cost at an institution that discovered mid-incident that backups hadn't been tested in 18 months and were partially corrupt: $500K–$3M in recovery, business interruption, and cyber-insurance coverage disputes.

The GLBA finding when access provisioning and deprovisioning was undocumented and terminated staff retained system access for weeks: $75K–$300K per finding, plus the administrative-capability determination that flows to FSA.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the five IT workflows on paper at audit-grade quality — with the runbook staff can find at 2 a.m.: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

GLBA Safeguards Rule (16 CFR 314) requires a written information-security program aligned to NIST 800-171 for federal student-aid data. The absence of one is a finding whether or not there was an incident.

The auditor doesn't inspect the wiring. They ask for the binder. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
