A community college I visited last spring had a bursar's assistant who was, effectively, the entire cash-application function. When a payment came in without a clean student match, she pulled up two spreadsheets, cross-referenced them against the merchant-processor log, and made the call.

Nobody had asked her to build that workflow. She'd inherited a piece of it from her predecessor and refined the rest herself over eleven years. It never made it to paper.

Then she retired. The next person had a title, a desk, and no way to reproduce the sequence. Within a month the aged-receivables queue had doubled. Within a quarter, an internal audit flagged three exceptions that had previously been resolved before they surfaced.

Your bursar's assistant is running a workflow that only survives if she does. The institutional memory lives in her. Nobody noticed because nobody asked.
