Three numbers to put against your procurement calendar.

The annual cost of stalled RFPs at a mid-size institution: $400K–$1.5M in strategic initiatives that didn't launch on schedule, donor relationships that didn't close, and programs that started six months late because the technology wasn't ready.

The vendor-pricing degradation between the sixty-day RFP cycle and the six-month RFP cycle: 5% to 15% on multi-year contracts — because the vendor either raises the quote to cover the extended sales cycle or your institution loses access to the pricing tier it originally qualified for.

The Uniform Guidance procurement-standards finding at institutions whose procurement workflow wasn't on paper against the 2 CFR 200 requirements: $50K–$250K per finding, plus the corrective action that requires re-competing the affected awards.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the four procurement workflows on paper — scoping, evaluation, negotiation, execution — with timing expectations and escalation routing: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200.318–.327) sets written-procedure requirements on federally-funded procurement. The requirement isn't for a policy statement. It's for a documented workflow, executed consistently, with the evidence trail the auditor can read.

Procurement acceleration is structural, not heroic. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
