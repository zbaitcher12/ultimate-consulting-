Three numbers to put against your audit calendar.

The liability exposure from a single Title IV program review finding tied to inadequate written procedures: $200K–$2M in returned funds, corrective action cost, and remediation staff time.

The duration of a corrective action plan resolution when the root cause is "we did the work but can't show the workflow": 6 to 18 months of active FSA engagement.

The administrative capability impact when repeat findings accumulate: heightened cash monitoring, provisional certification, or in the most escalated cases, participation loss.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the procedures federal reviewers ask for on paper — before they ask: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

GLBA is tested inside the annual single audit. Under 16 CFR 314, a written information-security program isn't optional — the absence of one is a finding whether or not there was an incident.

Federal reviewers don't inspect the wiring. They ask for the binder. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
