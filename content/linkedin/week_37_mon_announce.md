A mid-size private institution I worked with had a controller who could recite the internal-control framework from memory. Dual approval above twenty-five thousand. Segregation of duties on high-value transactions. Documented audit trails on the workflows that carried the highest exposure.

The framework lived in the controller's office. The operational documentation lived in the functional departments. The two portfolios overlapped substantially and were maintained separately.

Twice a year, the operational documentation drifted out of alignment with the control framework. A workflow updated to accommodate a new system. A signature threshold changed to reflect a delegated authority. The change didn't propagate to the controller's binder because the controller wasn't in the meeting.

The gap was invisible until an audit surfaced it. Every time, the finding read the same way — the control was documented, the workflow had drifted from it, evidence trail broken.

One portfolio, not two. That's what solved it.
