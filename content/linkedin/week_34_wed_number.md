Three numbers to put against your audit calendar.

The operational cost of event-mode audit prep at a mid-size institution: 6 to 12 weeks of paused work across financial aid, bursar, registrar, and IT — repeated on every scheduled review, plus every unscheduled one.

The finding count differential between event-mode institutions and institutions running the ongoing readiness flow: 3 to 8 additional preliminary findings on the event-mode side, on the same institution, on the same review scope — because the flow institution has evidence ready and the event-mode institution is reconstructing.

The FSA escalation cost when repeat findings accumulate into an administrative-capability determination: $200K–$1.5M in heightened cash monitoring costs, provisional certification remediation, or — in the most escalated cases — Title IV participation loss exposure.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to move one high-risk compliance area into the ongoing readiness flow: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

The full compliance chain runs: GLBA Safeguards Rule finding → single-audit finding → FSA administrative-capability determination → heightened cash monitoring → provisional certification → in escalated cases, participation loss. Each stage compounds the previous. Documentation is what interrupts the chain at the first stage.

The review that's uneventful is the review your institution prepared for over years, not weeks. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
