A public regional university president I worked with had made a bet in year one of her tenure that hadn't been visible in the strategic plan. She'd committed to putting the operating model on paper over a defined horizon as the priority that supported all the other priorities.

Five years in, the bet had produced compounding returns nothing else in the plan had matched. The audit exits were uneventful. The ERP cutover mid-tenure had landed without the eighteen-month cleanup her peers were still absorbing. Cross-functional decisions closed in weeks instead of quarters. Staff transitions no longer reset institutional knowledge.

The bet had never had a natural constituency. No faculty senate champion. No student newspaper interest. No donor naming opportunity. No board committee focused on it. Its benefits had compounded over a horizon no single budget cycle captured.

That was precisely why the bet had been undervalued when she'd made it. And precisely why it had worked.
