Three numbers worth pricing out before the next gap-analysis request.

The cost per finding on a six-month, institution-wide gap analysis at a mid-size institution: $8K–$25K per finding surfaced, of which typically 60% to 80% go unacted-on because the finished work arrived after the operating cycle had moved on.

The cost per finding on a two-week, single-workflow gap analysis: a fraction of the six-month figure, with acted-on rates of 70% or higher — because the finding arrives while the cabinet still remembers commissioning the work.

The discovery-documentation quality differential between the two cadences: the two-week cycle produces workflow-level notes that feed directly into the SOP; the six-month cycle produces synthesis abstractions that require re-capture when the SOP work eventually starts.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run one narrow-scope, two-week gap analysis on a single high-risk workflow — with a decision matrix as the finished output: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

The first-conversation record isn't a byproduct of the analysis. It's the input to the SOP work. When the analysis is fast enough that the notes stay fresh, the downstream documentation starts from a stronger foundation.

The document that lands is the document the operating cycle can still act on. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
