A mid-size private institution I worked with had a policy library it hadn't touched in four years. The documents had all been current at publication. Every one of them had drifted.

The refresh had been treated as an event — done when a document was written, done again when someone noticed it was stale. Between those two moments, nothing.

We built an annual cadence. Three triggers. Calendar — every document reviewed on a scheduled date. Change — any relevant system, regulation, or staffing shift kicks a refresh. Audit — a finding or program review triggers a full rewrite.

Most refreshes were lightweight. Confirm the workflow hadn't changed materially. Update the version date. Log the review. Full rewrites were reserved for material change.

Documentation isn't a one-time asset. It's a renewable one, and it renews on a cadence or it doesn't renew at all. The institutions whose portfolios compound over years run a cadence.
