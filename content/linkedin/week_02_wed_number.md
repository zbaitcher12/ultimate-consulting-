Three numbers to put against the project plan before your next retirement announcement.

The productivity loss when an experienced administrator retires from a function whose core workflows were never on paper: 6 to 9 months of reduced throughput while the successor reverse-engineers what the previous person knew.

The federal single-audit finding when Uniform Guidance-required written procedures aren't in place: $50K–$500K in corrective action cost, plus repeat findings that escalate to heightened cash monitoring.

The pre-implementation delay on any ERP or SIS project when institutions arrive with workflows that live only in memory: 30 to 60 days of workflow discovery before configuration decisions can even start.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to inventory the critical workflows across a single function — and identify which ones live only in one person's memory: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

The Safeguards Rule doesn't ask whether you have great staff. It asks whether their knowledge is written down in an information-security program. Uniform Guidance doesn't ask what your controls are. It asks for the procedure.

Every audit season, someone finds out the hard way that "we've always done it this way" isn't a control. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
