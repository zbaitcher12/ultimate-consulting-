Three numbers to put against your handoff calendar.

The operational cost of an undocumented aid-to-bursar handoff at a mid-size institution: $200K–$600K annually in manual reposting, student account holds, and the phone-tree work when a disbursement lands in a state neither office fully controls.

The annual audit exposure from the advancement-to-finance handoff when donor-restriction tracking wasn't jointly documented: $150K–$500K in gift-agreement compliance risk, plus the restatement work when the fund-accounting side and the donor-record side don't reconcile.

The registration-to-IT propagation-failure cost when the change routing wasn't on paper: 20 to 60 downstream system anomalies per term — schedule holds, financial aid awarding errors, credential-issuance gaps that surface as student complaints two weeks later.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the three highest-risk handoffs on paper with joint ownership, timing, and escalation routing: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Handoff documentation is where Uniform Guidance's written-procedure requirement meets the cross-functional workflows the auditor actually samples. "The other office owns that" isn't a documented handoff. It's the absence of one.

The handoff is where your institution actually lives. It's also where the finding lives. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
