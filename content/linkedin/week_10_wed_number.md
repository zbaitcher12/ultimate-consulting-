Three numbers to put against the next CIO transition.

The cost of reversing a locked-in ERP configuration decision made before the current CIO arrived: $300K–$1.2M per material decision in reconfiguration, integrator hours, and staff retraining — assuming reversal is even possible without triggering a downstream cascade.

The duration of the first-year rework at institutions where the incoming CIO discovered the configuration didn't match a documented workflow (or where the documented workflow didn't exist): 12 to 18 months of parallel operations, staff overtime, and deferred strategic work.

The GLBA finding at institutions in mid-stream migration when the incoming CIO inherits an incomplete written information-security program: $100K–$500K in remediation, plus an administrative-capability determination that flows to FSA.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to produce a CIO Operational Readiness Briefing before the transition — with the four areas named and the highest-risk items surfaced: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

GLBA Safeguards Rule requires a written information-security program with a qualified individual owning it. Under 16 CFR 314, the absence of one is a finding whether or not there was an incident — and mid-migration is the weakest moment an institution has to answer for it.

Moving to the cloud doesn't move the audit. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
