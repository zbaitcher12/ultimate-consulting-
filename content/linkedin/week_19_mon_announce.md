A community college I worked with had exited its last federal program review with three findings. The president had asked what it would take to exit the next one cleanly.

The team pulled together a compliance workstream. Binders assembled. Evidence indexed. Six weeks of scramble the month before the review opened.

The findings had been about consistency of workflow execution, not the absence of a policy. The scramble hadn't addressed the underlying pattern. The next review would find the same gaps because the operating cadence between reviews still didn't produce evidence as a byproduct.

The shift the president was actually asking for wasn't a bigger binder. It was integration — the Title IV disbursement workflow producing the disbursement evidence as it ran. The R2T4 procedure producing the R2T4 record without a parallel workstream.

Event-mode compliance produces binders. Operating-mode compliance produces uneventful reviews.
