A public regional university I worked with had commissioned documentation work the previous January that was supposed to land by summer. It landed in November.

By the time the finished document arrived, the cabinet that had funded it had turned over one member. The provost who'd sponsored it had moved to a new institution. The staff who'd sat through workflow interviews in February had cycled through two operational crises they no longer remembered discussing.

The document was technically fine. Institutionally it landed in an empty room.

The following spring we ran a different cycle. One workflow. Four weeks from the first conversation to the first audit-ready draft. The same cabinet that funded it in the first week saw the draft in the fourth. The staff who'd sat in the room recognized their own words in the pages.

Thirty days isn't a compression of six months. It's a different structure — narrow scope, tight participants, calibrated timeline.
