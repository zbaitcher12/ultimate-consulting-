A regional state system I worked across had leadership convinced that its documentation problem was one problem — the aid offices needed to write more SOPs.

Six weeks in, we'd found three different problems wearing the same label. One campus had tribal knowledge — decade-old workflows nobody had thought to write down because everyone who needed them already knew them. Another had configured-around exceptions — workarounds staff had built to route around a system quirk, workarounds that had become the actual workflow. A third had ghost approvals — decisions being made every week without a documented authority chain because the formal chain was too slow.

Each pattern needed a different fix. Treating them as one problem produced generic binders nobody used.

The categories weren't obvious until we stopped looking for missing documents and started asking why the documents were missing.
