A public regional university I worked with had a registrar's deputy who was the only person on staff who fully understood the residency-classification logic for a system that spanned three counties and five military bases.

She'd built the logic over eleven years — state statute, dependency rules, military exceptions, employer relationships, an appeals process that had accumulated its own conventions. None of it was on paper. The registrar's team ran her judgment through her when a case was complicated. When a case was routine, they applied her rules from memory.

She was six months from retirement when we met. The institution had not yet planned for the transition.

Residency wasn't the only workflow like that in the office. SAP appeals. Transfer credit evaluation. FERPA response routing. Each ran on judgment refined over years by a single person, none of it on paper.
