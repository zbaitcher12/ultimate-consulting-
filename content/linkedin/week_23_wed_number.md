Three numbers to put against your retention program.

The retention loss at a mid-size institution whose early-alert program lacks documented triage: 40 to 120 students per term who appeared on the dashboard, received uneven follow-up depending on which staff member picked up the flag, and did not return the following term.

The tuition and fee value of those students: $600K–$2.5M annually — the retention loss the program was funded to prevent.

The follow-up completion rate at institutions whose case-management workflow doesn't specify contact cadence: 30% to 50% of flagged students receive fewer than the intended number of touchpoints, because "who calls next" was never on paper and staff assumed someone else was handling it.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the four case management workflows on paper — triage, referral, follow-up, outcome documentation — at the level any staff member can run consistently: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Student services documentation isn't a compliance requirement in the same way Title IV documentation is. But the retention outcome your institution funded the program to produce depends on it just as much.

Retention dollars don't produce retention. Retention work does. The work runs on case management SOPs that distinguish what happens consistently from what happens by chance. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
