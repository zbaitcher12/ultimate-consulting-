Three numbers to put against the next rollout plan.

The delay between successful pilot completion and the next piece of work at institutions without a rollout governance structure: 6 to 18 months of drift — during which the pilot documentation ages, the momentum dissipates, and the cabinet moves to other priorities.

The institutional cost of stalled scale-out at a mid-size institution that documented one workflow and never got to the next: $250K–$750K annually in the ninety-nine other workflows that continued to produce turnover cost, audit exposure, and cross-functional friction the first workflow no longer produces.

The acceleration when the rollout governance is defined at pilot kickoff — with the maturity map, the pattern choice, and the next-piece gating criteria named up front: 3 to 6 months to launch the second piece instead of 6 to 18.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to name the rollout governance structure at pilot scoping — including which pattern your institution's maturity map indicates: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Rollout governance isn't a separate workstream. It's a scoping decision made at kickoff — before the pilot even launches.

Pilot success is the easy part. Scale is where the way your institution operates gets built. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
