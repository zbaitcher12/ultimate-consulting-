Three numbers to put against your registrar's calendar.

The annual tuition-appeal cost at institutions whose residency-classification logic lives in the deputy's memory: $150K–$500K in reclassifications, board-of-visitors correspondence, and legal review — most of it triggered by the first three exceptions the successor didn't know how to handle.

The Title IV liability from a single SAP finding when the appeal workflow wasn't on paper at the operational level: $100K–$400K per affected award year, plus the individual review of every packaged student in the same standing category.

The FERPA-response variance across a semester at institutions whose FERPA routing lives in one trained staff member: 20% to 40% of responses handled outside the documented consent-verification workflow — every one of them a legal exposure your institution absorbs.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the four registrar workflows on paper against operational reality — including the state-specific logic and the appeal routing: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Title IV documentation isn't only in the aid office. SAP is a registrar SOP. Transfer credit posture is a Title IV SOP. FERPA response is a Title IV SOP. Every one of them shows up in program review.

When the person who knows how it works walks out the door, the way it works walks out with them. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"
