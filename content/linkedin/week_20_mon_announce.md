A public regional university I worked with was seven months into an RFP the institution had needed to close in ninety days. The strategic initiative that depended on vendor selection had slipped by two quarters.

The procurement team walked me through what had happened. The scoping had taken six weeks because the operational owner, the IT owner, and the budget owner had never met to align. The evaluation had taken eight weeks because the committee had assembled without a rubric. The contract negotiation had stalled twice because the general counsel review chain wasn't on paper.

None of the delays were unusual. Each stage had lost time in a predictable way. The compounding was what had produced the seven-month cycle.

Institutions that close RFPs in sixty days aren't staffed better. They have the routing, the decision points, and the escalation paths on paper. That's most of the difference.
