A community college president I met with had asked her CFO to price documentation work three times over four years. Every quote had come back at a number that made the request look like overhead rather than investment.

The math wasn't wrong. It was the wrong math.

The pricing assumed labor-intensive delivery — long transcription cycles, months-long draft-review cadences, hourly rates calibrated to labor intensity. That model had been built to serve institutions with budgets large enough to absorb it. It didn't fit her.

The labor that produces the value in documentation work is in the operational judgment, the cross-functional reconciliation, and the calibration to institutional context. The transcription — the piece that had been driving the cost — has changed structurally in the last three years.

The structure she'd been quoted was a version of the work that no longer needs to exist. She'd never been offered the version that does.
