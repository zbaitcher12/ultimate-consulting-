A public regional university I worked with had used the same outside firm for eight years. The relationship had produced good work. It had also produced a pattern nobody had named — the institution kept needing the firm.

The Q3 review the president ran named it. The firm wasn't extracting the institution. The structure of the work had never included a handoff. Scope covered the write-up. Participants were staff who would exit the room when the work landed. The last piece of the arrangement was the document, with the ownership question deferred.

Capability-building work has a different structure. The last piece is the handoff to the named internal owner. Participants include the staff who will own the work after. Scope covers the transition.

The distinction isn't about firm motivation. Both patterns can be run in good faith. It's structural. And the president had never asked which pattern he'd been buying.
