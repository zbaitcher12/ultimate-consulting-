Three numbers to put against the next budget request.

The historical price point for a single-workflow documentation project at the deep-expertise end of the market: $50K–$150K over 8 to 16 weeks — a scope calibrated to R1 operating budgets, which is the budget size the deep-expertise firms grew around.

The current price point for a single-workflow documentation project under a rethought structure: a fraction of the historical, delivered in a 2 to 4 week cycle — a scope that fits the operating-budget line items most institutions use for strategic work.

The annual carrying cost of documentation work deferred under the historical scale: $300K–$1M per institution in turnover-productivity loss, audit-finding remediation, and cross-functional friction the deferred work would have prevented — compounding every year the structure didn't fit the operating budget.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run one piece of work under the current economics on the highest-priority workflow: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Institutional buying changes when the unit economics change. The CFO who saw the old scope as overhead sees the new one as a line-item investment. The audit exposure math finally works in favor of doing the work.

The economics that changed are the economics that opened the door. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
