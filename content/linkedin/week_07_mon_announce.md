A mid-size private institution I met with had an aid director who was, in real terms, worth about three hundred thousand dollars a year. Her salary was eighty-two.

The number came from a quiet exercise the CFO ran after she gave notice. He mapped the workflows she carried. He estimated the productivity gap during transition — three to six months at roughly forty percent of a salary for the affected positions. He added the audit exposure of the one workflow she'd built and never written down. He added the retention cost of the student-facing errors that would accumulate in the gap.

The number wasn't visible in any budget. It was distributed across four categories finance never aggregated.

Your aid director is worth something like that too. The arithmetic just hasn't been run yet.
