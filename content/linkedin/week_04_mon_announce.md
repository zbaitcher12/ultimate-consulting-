A public regional university I worked with had a routine federal program review last year. The reviewer didn't find fraud. Didn't find intentional non-compliance. Found something quieter and more expensive.

She asked the aid office to walk her through the R2T4 workflow. Staff walked her through it clearly. Then she asked for the documented evidence that the workflow had been followed on a specific case. The evidence lived across three inboxes, one shared drive, and the memory of a staff member on family leave.

The finding wasn't that the workflow was wrong. It was that the institution couldn't show the workflow had been run.

The remediation binder took six weeks to assemble. The workflow itself hadn't changed. The gap was that the operating cadence had never been built to produce evidence as a byproduct — only to produce the outcome.
