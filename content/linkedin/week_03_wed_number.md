Three numbers to put against the project plan before your next cabinet meeting.

The time from cross-departmental exception surfacing to resolution when decision authority isn't on paper: 8 to 12 business days per exception, escalated through 3 to 5 levels.

The student-facing exceptions per academic term at a mid-size institution that require cross-office coordination: 200 to 500. Multiply.

The cabinet-hour cost of decisions that never should have reached the cabinet: 4 to 8 senior leadership hours per week reclaimed when decision architecture is written down and staff know the escalation path.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to map the cross-functional decisions your institution actually makes — and name the owner for each: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance requires written procedures. It also requires an assigned responsible party. Auditors don't accept "we handle it case by case." They read the procedure and identify the owner.

Your cabinet's time is not the escalation path. It's what escalation looks like when there is no other path. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
