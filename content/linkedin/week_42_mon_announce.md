An HBCU president I worked with wanted a view of how the work actually runs that would fit into the two minutes she gave to any single briefing on a Tuesday.

The cabinet had been feeding her ten metrics. She retained none of them. Not because the metrics were bad, but because the volume made the pattern invisible.

We built a five-number view. Process coverage. Refresh currency. Governance owner depth. Cross-functional decision cycle time. Audit finding-to-close cycle. Five numbers. Four quarterly trend lines each.

The absolute levels mattered less than the trends. Rising coverage with flat currency told her one story. Flat coverage with rising decision cycle time told her another. She learned to read the combination the way she'd learned to read a cash-flow statement.

The five-number view survived the two-minute constraint. The ten-metric report hadn't. She'd been reading the same data for two years and had never held the pattern in her head.
