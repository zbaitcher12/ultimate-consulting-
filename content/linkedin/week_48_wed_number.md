Three numbers to put against your outside-help ledger.

The multi-year run-rate on a transactional outside relationship at a mid-size institution: $200K–$600K annually in ongoing retainer and follow-on pieces of work — with each year addressing roughly the same functional territory, because internal capability never accumulates.

The multi-year cumulative cost of a capability-transfer pattern on the same institution: significantly lower over three years, because each piece of work closes with internal ownership and the next piece addresses a different problem — different workflow, different function, different capability.

The internal-capability differential at year three: 5 to 8 documented workflows fully owned internally under the capability-transfer pattern, versus 1 to 2 workflows still requiring external maintenance under the transactional pattern.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to structure the first piece of work with capability transfer built into the scope — including named internal owner from week one and methodology handoff at close: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Maturity isn't measured in years of outside relationship. It's measured in what the internal team owns after the work closes. The relationship that builds capability looks different from the relationship that extends dependency.

The scope structure at kickoff predicts the outcome. Read the RFP for it. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
