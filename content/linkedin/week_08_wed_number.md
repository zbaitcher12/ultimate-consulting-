Three numbers to put against the cabinet calendar.

The resolution time for a cross-functional exception when decision authority isn't on paper: 6 to 12 business days, escalated through 3 to 5 levels, with staff time absorbed at every level.

The cabinet-hour drain from decisions that shouldn't have reached the cabinet: 5 to 10 senior leadership hours per week — hours reclaimed the moment the chain is written down and staff know the routing.

The annual cost of a bottleneck-owner pattern (one senior staff member is the documented owner of decisions the operational level should handle): $150K–$400K in unmeasured decision-latency drag on the functions waiting behind them.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to map the highest-frequency cross-functional decisions and name the actual owner for each: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance requires a responsible party. Not "we handle it case by case." Not "we escalate to the cabinet." A named owner, in writing, for each documented workflow — the person the auditor asks for by function.

Your cabinet's time is not the escalation path. It's what escalation looks like when there is no other path. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
