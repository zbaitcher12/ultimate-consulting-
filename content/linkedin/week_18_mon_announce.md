A regional state system I worked with had IT SOPs. Twenty-two of them. All current. All version-controlled.

They covered the wrong things.

The documented procedures walked through routine tasks staff already knew — password resets, printer provisioning, standard onboarding. The undocumented procedures were the high-stakes ones auditors and cyber underwriters now expect at audit-grade quality. Incident response. Change management. Access deprovisioning on staff departure. Data classification.

When the state system's insurance renewal came around, the underwriter asked for four specific runbooks. The team produced two, in rough form, and drafted the other two under deadline pressure over three weeks.

The SOPs weren't missing because the IT team was careless. They were missing because five years of shifting federal expectations had moved the goalposts, and nobody had run a cadence to check what had moved.
