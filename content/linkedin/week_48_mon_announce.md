A community college I worked with had been through four rounds with three different firms over five years. Each round had produced good work. Each round had also produced a longer list of things the institution now depended on the firm to maintain.

The president ran the math in year six. She'd spent about eight times what she'd budgeted originally. The institution had a lower internal capability to run its own operating-model work than it had when she started.

The pattern wasn't intentional. It was structural. Each round had been scoped to produce a document, with the ownership question deferred. Each round had ended with the outside firm knowing more about the work than the internal team. That created the case for the next round.

An arrangement designed for internal capability closes each round differently. The last piece is the handoff to the named internal owner. The next round, if there is one, addresses a different problem.
