Three numbers worth pricing out before the next documentation commission.

The cabinet-attention half-life on a strategic operating initiative once the first conversation has closed: 60 to 90 days. Past that window, the cabinet has moved to the next priority and the finished document arrives to a room that no longer remembers why it was commissioned.

The cost of a six-month documentation project whose finished document landed after the momentum window closed: $80K–$220K for a document that gets filed and never referenced — the classic shelf-binder failure mode.

The quick-win compliance gaps a single 30-day cycle can close on a mid-size institution's highest-risk workflow: 3 to 6 procedural gaps, 1 to 2 authority-chain clarifications, and an audit-evidence loop the workflow now closes without staff having to hunt for the trail.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run a 30-day cycle on a single high-risk workflow — and land the finished document inside the momentum window: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Thirty days is not compression of a six-month project into thirty days. It's a different structure — narrow scope, tight participants, calibrated output.

Documentation that lands inside the momentum window gets used. Documentation that lands after it gets filed. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
