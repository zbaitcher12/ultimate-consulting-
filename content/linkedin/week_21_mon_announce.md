An HBCU I worked with had a major-gifts officer who'd cultivated a seven-figure donor over five years. Every conversation lived in his head. His contact reports were three-line summaries that hinted at context his memory supplied.

Then he took a position at another institution.

The next major-gifts officer inherited the file. The file contained the pledge amount, the pledge schedule, and a phone number. The relationship — the cultivation choreography, the family dynamics, the reason the donor cared about the scholarship fund and not the capital campaign — went with the departing staff member.

The pledge closed at fifty cents on the dollar.

The SOPs that would have solved this don't templatize the cultivation. They document the pipeline infrastructure — contact reporting standards, moves-management stage definitions, portfolio-transition protocols — so the relationship-specific work has a documented container. The institution had never built that container.
