Three numbers to put against the next quarterly review.

The documented workflows a mid-size institution can close inside a single quarter when the three patterns are intact (naming, sponsorship, cadence): 3 to 5 high-risk workflows fully documented, owned, and refresh-scheduled.

The carrying cost, per quarter, of an operating-model initiative that has presidential sponsorship but no named executive owner: $60K–$150K in staff meeting time, outside retainer, and cabinet attention consumed without a finished document produced.

The lift in audit-season readiness at institutions whose Q1 documented workflows were the ones the fiscal-year audit sample would draw from: 2 to 4 fewer preliminary findings on the next single audit — before the corrective action cost the findings would have carried.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run the three patterns deliberately for one quarter on the highest-risk workflows: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Annual audit season doesn't announce itself six months in advance. The institutions that build documentation portfolios one quarter at a time are the institutions whose auditors sample workflows that already have current SOPs.

The quarter that compounds is the quarter that named, sponsored, and ran the cadence. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
