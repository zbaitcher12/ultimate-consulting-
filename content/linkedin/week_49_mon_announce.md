An MSI president I worked with had the operational profile where documented discipline compounds fastest. Title IV in the seventies as a share of revenue. Concentrated staffing — positions carrying multiple cross-functional workflows, which meant every documented step converted to throughput directly. Audit exposure tightly coupled to operating cycles.

She had priced operating-model help three times. Each quote had come back at a number calibrated to R1 budgets.

The gap wasn't about the firms she'd asked. It was structural. Firms that grew serving R1 institutions built cost structures that fit R1 budgets. Those cost structures didn't map to her engagement scale.

That gap is what the shift in engagement economics closes. Not through pricing tricks — through structuring the work differently so the depth is accessible at the engagement scale the operating budget actually uses.
