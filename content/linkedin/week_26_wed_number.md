Three numbers worth pricing out before mid-year audit prep.

The documented workflows a mid-size institution has across its ten most operationally important functions: 60% to 70% at the high-regulation functions (aid, IT for controls, controller), 20% to 35% at the low-regulation functions (marketing, advancement, student services, IR).

The cross-functional handoff drag when one function operates at 70% maturity and the receiving function operates at 25%: 3 to 6 additional findings per audit cycle sourced from the lower-maturity side of the handoff — even when the higher-maturity side did everything right.

The cost of a mid-year audit prep sprint at institutions that entered the second half without a documented map of the portfolio: 8 to 14 weeks of pulled cross-functional time to reconstruct what should have been documented as-you-go.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to produce the institution-wide maturity map — one half-day session with cabinet and functional leaders — before mid-year audit prep begins: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Mid-year audit prep is easier when the map already exists. The institutions that produce the map in June don't scramble in September.

Documentation maturity is uneven by design. The work that closes the unevenness is operating-model work, not functional work. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
