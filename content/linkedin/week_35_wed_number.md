Three numbers to put against the next reaffirmation cycle.

The bandwidth consumed by a reaffirmation cycle at a mid-size institution when operational documentation and accreditation evidence were maintained as parallel workstreams: 12 to 24 months of pulled staff time across every function, with roughly 60% of the effort spent assembling evidence that already existed in operational form but wasn't recognized as accreditation-ready.

The duplicative-documentation cost at institutions maintaining two portfolios (operational and accreditation-specific): $150K–$400K annually in redundant assembly, version-reconciliation, and evidence-tracking work.

The monitoring-report burden at institutions whose reaffirmation exit carried substantive change or focus-report requirements sourced from evidence-package gaps: 2 to 5 years of additional reporting, at $50K–$150K annually in staff time and outside fees.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run one evidence-mapping session between accreditation and operating-model leadership — with the three structural moves named: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

SACSCOC, HLC, and Middle States standards all contain evidence requirements operational documentation addresses. When the accreditation liaison sits in operational governance and the operational documents cite the accreditation requirements they satisfy, one portfolio does both jobs.

The conversation that connects the two is the conversation that produces the integration. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
