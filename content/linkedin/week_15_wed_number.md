Three numbers to put against your bursar's calendar.

The cash-application aging at institutions whose bursar exception-handling relies on staff memory rather than a documented workflow: 3 to 12 days of payments sitting unposted, driving student account holds, retention calls, and inbound student inquiries the front desk absorbs.

The annual write-off from refund-processing errors when the refund SOP names the policy but not the routing: $80K–$350K in re-issued refunds, escheatment complications, and the manual research to find where the first refund went.

The Title IV credit-balance finding at institutions whose credit-balance workflow isn't on paper against the current federal timing requirements: $50K–$200K per finding, plus the disbursement-timing corrective action that runs for the next four to six terms.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the six bursar workflows on paper at operational — not policy — depth: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

The Uniform Guidance cash-management standard doesn't ask what the bursar's policy is. It asks how the exception was resolved, by whom, and against what documented workflow. The exception queue is the answer — either it names a documented routing or it names improvisation.

Your exception queue is your measurement instrument. It tells your institution what to fix. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
