Three numbers to put against your onboarding calendar.

The productivity gap between orientation and independent execution at a mid-size institution: 90 to 180 days of sub-productive time per new hire, at 30% to 50% of the fully-loaded salary — absorbed by your institution as normal staffing cost.

The annual value of the documentation observations a new hire could contribute in their first 90 days — if the onboarding structure explicitly invited them: 3 to 8 workflow-level gaps identified per hire, each worth $10K–$50K when addressed before the next successor absorbs them.

The acceleration to independent productivity when the new hire onboards against documentation that matches operational reality: 30 to 60 days faster, because the sidebar-coaching load drops.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to restructure onboarding to include the 30/60/90-day documentation review — with supervisor participation: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Documentation isn't only a compliance tool. It's an onboarding tool. The new hire who joins a function with current documentation reaches productivity faster and — if your institution captures the input — improves the documentation at the same time.

The new hire's first ninety days are an institutional learning window. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
