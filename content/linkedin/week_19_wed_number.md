Three numbers to put against the next program review.

The operational cost of event-mode program review preparation at a mid-size institution: 6 to 12 weeks of pulled staff time across financial aid, bursar, registrar, and IT — with the other work paused, deferred, or absorbed as overtime.

The preliminary finding count at institutions running event-mode prep versus operating-mode integration: 3 to 8 additional findings on the same institution, on the same review, because event-mode assembly can't retroactively show workflows staff didn't run consistently.

The FSA administrative-capability escalation cost when a repeat-finding classification lands: $200K–$1.2M in heightened cash monitoring costs, provisional-certification remediation, and — in the most escalated cases — participation-loss exposure that puts the whole Title IV program at risk.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to move one high-risk compliance area from event mode to operating mode: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

FSA doesn't grade the binder assembled the week before the review. It grades administrative capability. Repeat findings flow to FSA as capability determinations — heightened cash monitoring, then provisional certification, then in the most escalated cases participation loss.

The program review that's uneventful is the review your institution prepared for over years, not weeks. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
