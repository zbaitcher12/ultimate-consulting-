Three numbers to put against your advancement calendar.

The revenue impact when a senior gift officer departs and the prospect portfolio she carried can't be reassigned cleanly because contact reports were sparse and stewardship history lived in her email: 12 to 24 months of stalled cultivation on the six- and seven-figure prospects she was carrying, at $500K–$2M in deferred or lost gifts.

The cost of rebuilding donor relationships from a blank slate when the reassigned staff member had no cultivation history to inherit: 18 to 36 months of relationship rebuild before the prospect returns to the cultivation stage the prior officer had reached.

The annual cost of undocumented stewardship on named-gift donors: $100K–$500K in donor attrition on the second and third gifts that never come because the impact reporting slipped through the transition.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the four advancement workflows on paper at the infrastructure level — without constraining what officers actually say in a cultivation conversation: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Development office documentation isn't about templating the ask. It's about protecting the prospect portfolio your institution funded in the first place. The four workflows document the infrastructure, not the artistry.

Change that isn't written down is a rumor. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
