Three numbers to put against the budget before the next retirement.

The productivity loss at a mid-size institution when staff members carrying workflows on memory leave in a normal year: $200K annually, distributed across three departures and rebuild time in each successor's first six months.

The remediation cost of a single avoidable federal single-audit finding tied to a missing written procedure: $50K–$300K in outside fees, staff time, and Title IV liability, before the repeat-finding classification lands on the next cycle.

The post-go-live cleanup cost at institutions that exited cutover with technical debt from pre-migration workflows that only lived in memory: high six figures over 18 to 24 months.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to fund documentation on the highest-risk workflows: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Single-audit findings don't stay in the audit. They flow to FSA as administrative-capability determinations. Repeats escalate to heightened cash monitoring, provisional certification, or participation loss.

Every audit finding is really just a missing piece of paper.

The case for funding this work compounds faster at institutions where operations are more concentrated — every documented workflow couples directly to the next cash cycle and audit review. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
