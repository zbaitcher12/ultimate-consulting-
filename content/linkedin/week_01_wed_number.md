Three numbers to put against the project plan before the next cutover window.

The cost of extending ERP implementation by 90 days because pre-migration workflows weren't on paper: $400K–$1.2M in additional integrator hours and internal staff overtime.

The aid-disbursement delay when the new SIS goes live before the aid workflow it references has been documented: 5 to 14 days of packaged aid sitting undisbursed while staff reconstruct the process.

The productivity gap in the first six months post-go-live when staff have been trained on the system but not on the underlying workflow: 30% to 40% throughput reduction across affected functions.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the workflows the new system will configure around on paper, before configuration begins: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200) requires written procedures. Auditors test for the document itself. An undocumented process is a finding by definition, and a system going live doesn't change that.

Cutover doesn't produce institutional readiness. It surfaces whether the readiness was there.

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
