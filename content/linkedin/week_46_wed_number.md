Three numbers to put against your enrollment funnel.

The application-acknowledgment cycle-time differential between institutions with documented workflows and peers with improvised routing: 5 to 15 days per applicant. On an application pool of 3,000, that's a competitive melt of 3% to 8% — students your institution had reached but who committed elsewhere before the acknowledgment landed.

The enrollment yield loss at institutions whose acceptance-to-deposit cycle takes 4 to 8 weeks longer than peers with documented workflows: 100 to 300 students per class, at $15K–$30K in tuition and fees per student — meaning $2M–$9M annually walking to competitors.

The pre-term readiness gap at institutions whose deposit-to-enrollment workflow lives in staff memory across six offices: 15% to 30% of deposited students arriving on day one without complete housing, aid, or registration setup — driving the summer-melt losses that show up in fall census.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the four funnel workflows on paper at operational depth — with joint ownership across the offices they cross: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Cycle-time compliance isn't a slogan. It's the discipline that lets your institution respond at the pace applicants expect — before the competitor with the shorter cycle wins them. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
