A community college I worked with had spent two years building an early-alert system. New advising structures. Supplemental instruction. A care team. All the program dollars in the right places.

Retention had not moved.

We spent a week watching what happened after a flag appeared on the alert dashboard. Sometimes an advisor called within twenty-four hours. Sometimes it was five days. Sometimes the flag closed with no documented outreach at all. The variation wasn't by student. It was by which advisor picked up the case, and how their day had gone.

The program structures funded the people who did the work. The workflows the people ran had never been written down. Triage rules were improvised. Referral routing was informal. Documentation of closure was optional.

Retention dollars had bought the seats. The workflows between the touchpoints — where retention actually lives — had been left to individual judgment.
