A mid-size private institution I worked with had an HR director who kept every termination protocol in her head. She'd handled seventeen of them cleanly over eight years. The institution had a policy binder. The binder covered onboarding, benefits, and the annual review cycle. It didn't cover progressive discipline, Title IX response routing, or the sequence for a for-cause termination.

Then the eighteenth termination went to litigation. Discovery asked for the documented protocol. Your institution didn't have one.

The protocol had worked seventeen times because she'd been the one running it. Once the case reached court, the question wasn't whether the outcome had been fair. The question was whether the institution could show a consistent workflow.

HR workflows are the ones institutions defer writing down until the moment they're being defended in court. The deferral has an obvious reason. It also has a predictable cost.
