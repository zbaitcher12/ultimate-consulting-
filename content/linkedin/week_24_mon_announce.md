A mid-size private institution I worked with had brought in a new chief marketing officer. Within six months she'd commissioned a brand refresh. The standards guide was thoughtful. The launch was on schedule.

She was the fourth CMO in twelve years to run the same cycle.

Each prior CMO had arrived, observed inconsistency across channels, commissioned a refresh, published a standard, and left before the standard had time to embed. Each next CMO had inherited drift and started over.

The problem wasn't the quality of any single refresh. It was that the institution had never captured brand execution at the level of operational detail that survives staff transitions. Campaign briefs. Channel playbooks. The workflow for translating a strategic objective into a scored campaign specification.

The standards guide was the visible artifact. The operational infrastructure underneath it wasn't on paper, so the standards decayed at the pace of CMO tenure.
