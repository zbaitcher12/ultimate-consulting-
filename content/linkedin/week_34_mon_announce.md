An HBCU I worked with ran audit prep in event mode. Two months out from a review, the team stood up a focused readiness push. Six months out, the readiness portfolio was quieter. The reviews exited clean — the team had built the discipline to produce clean exits — but the cycle was tied to review proximity rather than to ongoing operating rhythm.

The leadership team made the decision to move audit readiness into ongoing flow. Five stages running continuously. Identification — naming which workflows need audit-ready documentation. Documentation — producing the write-ups. Evidence-mapping — connecting each document to the audit requirements it addresses. Peer review — internal review from outside the originating function. Refresh — the cadence that keeps the whole portfolio current.

The first three were already running on review proximity. The last two were the shift. Converting event-mode into flow was less work than one more review cycle. The institution had the discipline. The restructure let the discipline run year-round.
