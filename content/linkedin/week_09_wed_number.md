Three numbers to put against the next gap-analysis budget request.

The cost of a six-month, institution-wide gap analysis at a mid-size institution: $80K–$200K in outside fees and internal staff time — for a finished document that arrives after the operating cycle has moved on and typically produces no decision.

The carrying cost of the gaps a stale analysis surfaced but the cabinet never acted on: $250K–$1M per year in audit remediation, exception handling, and cross-functional friction the gaps continue to produce.

The federal program review finding on an institution that has "done gap analysis" but hasn't closed the gaps: identical to the finding at an institution that never ran the analysis at all. Reviewers don't grade the finished document. They test the workflow.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run narrow-scope, two-week gap analysis on one workflow — with a decision matrix as the output: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Federal program review posture is not about whether your institution has analyzed the gaps. It's about whether the gaps were closed. Auditors don't ask for the analysis. They ask for the procedure.

The document nobody uses is the document that arrived too late to be useful.

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
