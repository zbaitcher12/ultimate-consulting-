An HBCU I worked with had traced graduation-rate variance for six years. The variance patterns had been treated as a data problem — new programs, new advising models, new interventions.

We spent a week walking the actual student-journey workflows. The financial-aid workflow took longer for students whose verification documents arrived in non-standard format. The advising workflow produced consistent guidance for students whose situations fit the workflow and improvised guidance for students whose situations did not. The student-services routing case-managed effectively when escalations were loud and lost students whose signals were subtle.

The variance wasn't primarily a data problem. It was a workflow gap. Undocumented workflows concentrate harm on students whose situations don't fit the assumed path, because those are precisely the students improvised workflows serve unevenly.

The programmatic investments had been real. They'd been layered on top of workflow gaps that were quietly redistributing the outcomes the programs were meant to produce.
