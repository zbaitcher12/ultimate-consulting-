Three numbers to put against your refresh calendar.

The drift between documented workflow and operational reality at institutions with no refresh cycle: 30% to 50% of documented steps out-of-alignment within 18 months of original publication — enough drift that new hires trained against the documentation still need sidebar coaching.

The cost of a full rewrite triggered because a workflow was allowed to drift for three or more years: $30K–$100K per workflow — compared with $3K–$10K for a lightweight refresh had the cadence been maintained.

The audit exposure at institutions that couldn't produce the version-in-force at the date of the transaction the reviewer asked about: $75K–$300K per affected finding — because "we've updated it since then" is not a response the reviewer accepts.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run one annual refresh cycle across your institution's top ten load-bearing workflows: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

The documentation lifecycle isn't a bureaucratic add-on. It's the difference between documentation that stays load-bearing and documentation that decays into decoration. The lightweight refresh is what keeps the full rewrite from becoming necessary.

Documentation that doesn't refresh becomes decoration. The cadence is what keeps it load-bearing. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
