Three numbers to put against your next documentation commission.

The failure rate of documentation projects at institutions where scope was set at function-wide or institution-wide rather than at workflow-specific: 60% to 80% of finished documents end up unused within twelve months of delivery — because the scope was too broad to produce documentation staff could act on.

The sunk-cost hit on documentation work that closed without a defined internal owner: $75K–$250K per project, distributed across outside fees and internal staff hours, with no residual operational change because ownership defaulted to nobody.

The lift in "in-use at 12 months" rate at institutions where the five upstream decisions were made deliberately versus left to drift: 3x to 5x higher — meaning the same dollar of investment produces three to five times the operational return.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run the five-decision diagnostic before kickoff — and adjust scope, sponsor, or governance where the answers indicate: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Change management framing for documentation work isn't about training or communication. It's about the five upstream decisions that determine whether the finished document becomes a load-bearing part of operations — or decoration nobody references.

Most projects fail before they start. The diagnostic is how to be the exception. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"
