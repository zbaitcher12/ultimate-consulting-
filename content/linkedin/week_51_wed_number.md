Three numbers to put against the year-end calendar.

The bandwidth available for portfolio work in November and December compared with any other two months in the operating year: 2 to 3 times more, at a mid-size institution — because fall term is closing, spring hasn't started, and the audit is in field work rather than institutional response.

The institutional cost of skipping the year-end refresh cycle for two or more consecutive years: 40% to 60% of the portfolio drifts out of alignment, at $30K–$100K per workflow in eventual full-rewrite cost — versus $3K–$10K per workflow if the lightweight refresh had been run on cadence.

The next-year benefit of running the five-component December cadence: the operating year opens with a current portfolio, a named gap queue, and no last-minute audit-response reconstruction — clearing 4 to 8 weeks of staff time in the following spring.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run the five-component cadence across the top twenty load-bearing workflows in November and December: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Annual cycle close is not just an accounting exercise. It's a portfolio exercise. The institutions that use the December window preserve the investments of prior years and queue the work next year requires. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"
