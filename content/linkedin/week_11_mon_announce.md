A regional state system I worked with had spent nine months producing an SOP binder. Fifty documents. Cross-referenced. Version-controlled. Handed over on time.

Six months later a chancellor's-office scan found that fewer than a dozen of the fifty were being used. The rest sat unopened on a shared drive.

The gap wasn't the quality of the binder. It was the quality of the underlying work. The documents had been written by supervisors who thought they knew how the workflows ran. The staff who actually did the work hadn't been the source. The exceptions weren't in the documents. The workarounds weren't named. The evidence trails weren't built into the workflow — they were parallel to it.

A binder that satisfies the immediate request is not the same as documentation that changes operations. The first is a compliance product. The second is different work, captured from different people, held to a different standard.
