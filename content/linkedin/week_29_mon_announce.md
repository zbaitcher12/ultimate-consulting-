A mid-size private institution I worked with had a gap analysis from three years earlier sitting on the CFO's shelf. Sixty pages. Every finding still accurate. Not one had been acted on.

The findings had arrived four months after the request. By then the operating cycle had moved on. The cabinet that commissioned the work had reset priorities. The analysis got filed.

We ran a smaller version the next month. Two-week cycle. One cross-functional workflow — the aid-to-bursar disbursement handoff. Findings arrived while the cabinet still remembered commissioning the work.

Three categories surfaced. A workflow gap where the documented workflow didn't match execution. An authority gap where nobody owned the decision when the disbursement timing was off. A measurement gap where nobody could tell how often the handoff was failing until we counted.

The cabinet acted on all three inside the same operating quarter. That had never happened with the sixty-page version.
