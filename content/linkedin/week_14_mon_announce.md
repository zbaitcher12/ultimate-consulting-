An HBCU I worked with had an aid office that ran like a compliance machine. Every workflow on paper. R2T4. SAP. Cash-management. Disbursement routing. When the federal reviewer came, she found evidence assembled as a byproduct of normal operations, not as a scramble.

Two floors up, the same institution's institutional-research function ran on one staff member's memory. Data definitions in her head. Dashboard logic in her head. IPEDS crosswalks in a spreadsheet only she could interpret.

The differential wasn't about competence. It tracked regulatory pressure. Aid documented because Title IV made the discipline non-optional. IR didn't, because nothing external forced it.

The aid director had built discipline the rest of the institution could adopt without inventing it. Nobody had asked her to teach it. Nobody in IR, marketing, or advancement had thought to ask what she was doing that they weren't.
