A community college I visited in April was closing out its first quarter of intentional operating-model work. The cabinet asked what had produced the visible progress.

Three things. None of them were budget.

They'd named the challenge specifically. Not operating discipline in general. Title IV documentation maturity in the aid office. Decision-authority clarity for cross-departmental exceptions. Audit-readiness posture for the SACSCOC visit two years out. The naming produced work. Generic naming had produced conversation.

They'd assigned a single executive sponsor with defined scope. Presidential sponsorship alone had produced nothing in the previous fiscal year because everything landed on the president's plate. The moment the VP of student affairs took explicit ownership, the workstreams started converging.

They'd stopped treating documentation as a one-time write-up. The first thirty days included the naming of the internal owner who'd carry the work in month thirteen.
