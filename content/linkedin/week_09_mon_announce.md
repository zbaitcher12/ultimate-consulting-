A community college I worked with had commissioned a gap analysis eighteen months earlier. The result was a hundred-page slide deck. Nobody had opened it since.

The dean who'd sponsored it pulled it up when we sat down. The findings named gaps in institution-wide language — process maturity, documentation coverage, decision architecture. Every finding was true. Not one was actionable.

We ran a different version the next week. One cross-functional workflow. The staff who actually ran it in the room. A findings-to-decisions matrix instead of a findings list. What to close in ninety days, what to close in six months, what to leave alone.

The write-up was fourteen pages. The dean acted on it in the same operating quarter.

The first analysis had been generic enough to produce conversation. The second was specific enough to produce work. That was the only difference.
