Three numbers to put against the controller's calendar.

The cost of maintaining two parallel documentation portfolios — an internal-control framework in the controller's office and an operational SOP set in the departments: $150K–$400K annually in duplicative version-reconciliation, cross-office review, and audit-response reconstruction.

The annual fraud loss at institutions where the segregation-of-duties requirement existed in policy but wasn't on paper at the operational workflow level: $75K–$500K in vendor-setup fraud, payroll-separation errors, and refund-diversion cases the documented workflow would have caught.

The finding cost from a Uniform Guidance internal-control review when the control existed but couldn't be traced to the operational document that executed it: $50K–$200K per finding, plus the corrective action that requires rebuilding the trace.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to integrate the two portfolios — control language inside the operational documents, controller participation in operational governance: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance requires internal controls sufficient to provide reasonable assurance that federal awards are managed in compliance with federal statutes, regulations, and terms. The controls aren't policy statements. They're documented workflows the auditor tests.

One set of documents. Two audiences. Half the maintenance cost. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
