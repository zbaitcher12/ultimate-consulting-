A public regional university I worked with had gone live on a new ERP two years earlier. The project team had reassigned at go-live plus six months. The system had been declared complete.

The twenty-four months since had drifted rather than been planned. Staff had developed workarounds when the system didn't handle a real-world case. The workarounds had become permanent. The configuration that was supposed to be optimized over the window had been worked around instead.

By the time we met, the institution had the new system, the workarounds, and the operating model it had gone live with — layered on top of each other, running in parallel, producing the exact inherited-mess pattern the modernization had been meant to resolve.

The optimization window wasn't a bonus. It was the phase where the value of the whole investment either compounded or evaporated. The institutions that plan the window get the value. The ones that drift into it don't.
