Three numbers to put against the CIO dashboard.

The annual cost of configuration decisions that don't match how operations actually run: $400K–$1.5M in workarounds, manual bridges, and shadow systems staff build to keep operations moving around the misaligned configuration.

The incident count from unauthorized changes at institutions whose change-management adherence isn't tracked as a distinct metric: 8 to 20 production incidents per year traceable to changes that skipped the documented workflow — each incident absorbing IT staff time that should have gone to strategic work.

The GLBA and single-audit finding count from document-to-system traceability gaps: 3 to 6 findings per audit cycle where the auditor asked which system executes the documented control and the IT team couldn't produce the trace.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to add the three configuration-to-workflow measures to the existing CIO dashboard: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

IT operational metrics beyond uptime aren't optional. They're what tell the CIO whether the technology landscape aligns with how your institution actually runs — or whether staff are absorbing the misalignment as normal daily work.

System uptime is a baseline. Configuration-to-workflow alignment is the strategic measure. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
