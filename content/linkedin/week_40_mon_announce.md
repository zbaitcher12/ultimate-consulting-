A regional state system I worked with had a board scorecard with eleven metrics. Enrollment. Financial position. Fundraising performance. Academic outcomes. Each one standardized. Each one discussed at every board meeting.

The record of how the work actually runs wasn't on the scorecard. The board heard about it only when something had broken — an audit finding, a system failure, a personnel matter that had revealed a gap.

Three indicators would have made it boardroom-ready. Process coverage — the percentage of identified load-bearing workflows with current documentation. Refresh currency — the percentage of the portfolio kept alive under a cadence. Governance owner depth — how many documents had a named internal owner with active refresh authority.

The board that monitors enrollment without monitoring the record is watching the symptom without watching the leading indicator. The board that adds these three isn't adding overhead. It's closing a measurement gap that had been quietly compounding.
