A regional state system I worked with had documented a hundred workflows over two years. Eighteen months later, seventy of the documents had drifted out of alignment with operational reality.

The drift wasn't sabotage. Systems had updated. Regulations had shifted. Staff had turned over. The workflows had continued to evolve. The documents hadn't.

Nobody had been named as the owner. No refresh cadence had been set. No change-control workflow had been built. The write-up had been treated as a finish line rather than the start of a life cycle.

Three governance models work. Process-owner — each document has a named individual with refresh authority. Governance committee — a standing group reviews the portfolio on a cadence. Embedded ops — the document lives inside the operational team that runs the workflow, refreshed as part of the operating cycle.

The system had chosen none of the three. The documents decayed at the pace institutions always predict when governance is absent.
