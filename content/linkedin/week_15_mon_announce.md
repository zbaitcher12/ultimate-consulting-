A community college I worked with kept a running exception queue in the bursar's office. Not by choice. By necessity. Cash-application mismatches. Merchant-processor batches that didn't reconcile. Refund routing that stalled at the second signature.

I asked the bursar how many items were in the queue that morning. She said about eighty. I asked how many had been there the same week the previous year. She said about eighty.

The size wasn't the diagnostic. The composition was. Roughly seventy of the eighty repeated every term, from predictable upstream causes nobody had ever written a workflow to catch. The other ten were genuinely new.

Her staff was spending most of a full-time position resolving exceptions that would have been prevented if the upstream workflows had been on paper. The queue wasn't a bursar problem. It was a symptom of documentation gaps in six other functions.
