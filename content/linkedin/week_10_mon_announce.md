A mid-size private institution I worked with had a new CIO who'd been in the seat for six weeks when we talked. He was already inheriting the pattern his three predecessors had inherited before him.

The ERP had gone live two years earlier. The configuration decisions had been made by an outside team that had since moved on and a CIO who'd departed the year after cutover. The staff who could have explained the gap between the configured workflow and the actual workflow had been excluded from the configuration discussions. The gap had calcified.

He wasn't looking for a rescue. He was looking for a briefing — the status the institution should have produced for him before day one but hadn't.

Your new CIO walks into the same pattern unless the briefing exists before the transition. Most institutions don't run that cycle.
