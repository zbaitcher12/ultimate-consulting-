Three numbers to put against your aid office's R2T4 calendar.

The liability from a single R2T4 calculation finding when the procedure existed but didn't match how staff actually ran the calculation: $75K–$300K per affected award year, plus a Title IV return your institution funds out of operating dollars.

The corrective action cost when Satisfactory Academic Progress documentation was in place at the policy level but not at the operational-workflow level: $50K–$250K in review, appeal, and case-by-case remediation across the affected term.

The verification-workflow error rate at institutions whose verification SOP hadn't been refreshed against the current federal specifications in more than 18 months: 3% to 8% of verified files re-verified in-cycle, with the corresponding disbursement delay and staff overtime.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to refresh the three highest-volume aid procedures — and adopt the same four principles across one other function: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

R2T4, SAP, and verification are what happens when the regulator makes the discipline non-optional. Every function that doesn't carry the regulator makes the same choice by default — and pays for it on a different line item.

Aid offices document because they have to. Other functions document because they've watched what happens when they don't. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
