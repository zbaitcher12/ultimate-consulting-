A public regional university I worked with had spent three years building documented cybersecurity controls. The trigger had been the cyber-insurance renewal in year one that had asked for evidence the institution didn't have.

Six weeks into the build, the CIO noticed something. The controls being asked for were operational workflows the institution already ran. Change management. Access provisioning. Incident response. Data handling. Just captured at a level of detail IT operations had never formalized.

The cybersecurity build was largely a documentation exercise applied to IT operations.

The institution's real opportunity wasn't producing two portfolios. It was capturing once and complying twice. The documents that described IT operations were the same documents that satisfied cybersecurity audits. Splitting the portfolios doubled the maintenance work and produced version drift between them.

The build converged on a single portfolio in year two. Maintenance dropped by half. Compliance improved.
