A mid-size private institution I worked with had spent three years optimizing conversion rates in the enrollment funnel. Inquiry-to-application. Application-to-acceptance. Acceptance-to-deposit. The team had moved every rate meaningfully.

Cycle time hadn't moved. That was the number applicants were feeling.

The institution took three weeks to acknowledge an application. Peer institutions were doing it in three days. The deposited students had seven separate enrollment steps to complete over six weeks. Peers were integrating those steps and closing them in two.

Cycle time is a function of what's on paper. Four documentation categories drove most of the variance. Application processing. Acceptance package generation. Enrollment step integration. Handoff routing between the enrollment team and the academic units.

The conversion-rate optimization had produced marginal gains inside a funnel whose total cycle time was still losing applicants to faster institutions. That was where the yield had actually been leaking.
