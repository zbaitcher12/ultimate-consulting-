Three numbers to put against your outside-help ledger.

The multi-year cost of a dependency-extending pattern at a mid-size institution: $300K–$1M cumulative over three years of retainer and follow-on work — with roughly the same operational workflows requiring roughly the same documentation work at each cycle, because internal capability never accumulated.

The multi-year cost of a capability-building pattern on the same institution: significantly less over three years, because the internal team took ownership after each piece of work and each subsequent piece addressed a different problem rather than maintaining the prior one.

The operating-capability differential at year three: 3 to 5 documented workflows fully owned internally under the capability pattern, versus 1 to 2 workflows still requiring outside support under the dependency pattern.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to check the five markers at RFP evaluation — before signing the letter: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Outcomes are predictable. The scope structure at kickoff predicts the capability outcome at closure. The institutions that read the RFP for the five markers commission work that compounds. The institutions that don't commission work that recurs.

The work that ends is the work that built capability. The work that transitions to retainer built dependence. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
