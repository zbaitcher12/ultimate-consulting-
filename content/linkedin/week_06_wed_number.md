Three numbers worth pricing out before the next operating year.

The liability across a single audit season at an institution whose Title IV documentation hasn't kept pace with a tightening program review cadence: $300K–$1.5M in corrective action, cash monitoring impact, and remediation staff time.

The cost of an AI pilot deployed on top of an undocumented workflow: $150K–$400K in rework once the pilot exits proof-of-concept and staff discover the workflow the AI was trained against was not the one being run.

The carrying cost of one load-bearing workflow at an institution that lost the staff member who ran it — and cannot backfill because enrollment loss removed the budget: 30% to 50% of the departed salary in productivity gap, running for 12 to 18 months.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to put the operating model on paper so it absorbs all three pressures: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance requires written procedures. Every audit season, an institution somewhere finds out that "we've always known how this works" isn't what the auditor asks for.

You can't put smart tools on top of a workflow that only lives in memory. Put it on paper first. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
