A public regional university I worked with had run a documentation project on a six-week cycle from first interviews to first draft. By the time the draft landed, one participating dean had left, the aid director had been pulled into a state audit, and the CFO had reset the fiscal-year priorities.

The draft was fine. The institutional context had moved.

We tried a different cadence on the next workflow. Two weeks from the first conversation to the first audit-ready draft. The staff who sat in the initial interviews saw the draft while they still remembered the interviews. The cabinet that funded the work in week one saw the draft in week three.

Two weeks isn't a compression of six. It's a different structure. Narrow scope — one workflow, one function, one specific gap. Tight participant list. Two hours of structured interviews rather than eight weeks of iterative sessions.

The compression is possible because the sprawl was never load-bearing to begin with.
