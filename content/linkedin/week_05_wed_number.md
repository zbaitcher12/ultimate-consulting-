Three numbers worth pricing out before the next audit cycle.

The productivity gap when a staff member carrying five to seven workflows on memory takes a position at another institution: 4 to 8 months of reduced throughput while the successor rebuilds the workflows from scratch.

The post-migration cleanup cost at institutions that migrated a system without capturing the workarounds staff had built to keep the prior system functioning: $250K–$800K in reconfiguration, integrator hours, and manual bridge work.

The federal single-audit finding when decisions were being made without a documented authority chain: $75K–$400K in corrective action, plus a repeat-finding classification that lands on the next audit cycle.

None of these are theoretical. I've watched all three at real institutions.

The investment required to run the inventory that surfaces which staff carry which workflows — and which category each one falls into: a fraction of any one of those numbers.

Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200) requires written procedures. The requirement doesn't distinguish between the workflow that's been running the same way for ten years and the one invented last month to work around a broken integration. If it's not written down, it's a finding.

Every institution has all three categories. The question is which one surfaces first. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"

Where would those numbers land at your institution?
