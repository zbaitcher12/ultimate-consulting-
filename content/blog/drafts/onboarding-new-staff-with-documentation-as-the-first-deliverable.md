The first ninety days of a new staff member's tenure in a higher-ed function typically follow a predictable pattern. The new hire receives orientation. The new hire shadows existing staff. The new hire begins executing work under supervision. The new hire becomes independently productive somewhere between ninety days and six months.

Your institution absorbs the productivity gap as a normal cost of staffing.

---

## The pattern misses an institutional learning window.

The new staff member sees the function's workflows for the first time. The new hire sees what is unclear, what is undocumented, what does not match the verbal explanation, what requires sidebar coaching that was not in orientation.

Within thirty days, the new hire has a clearer view of where the function's documentation is inadequate than the existing staff who have absorbed the inadequacies into normal practice. The view fades within ninety days as the new hire absorbs the gaps into normal practice.

---

## An onboarding model that captures the learning.

The institutions that capture the learning structure the new staff member's first ninety days to produce two outputs.

**The new hire becomes independently productive.** The conventional onboarding goal.

**The new hire contributes to documentation improvement.** A documented list of where the existing documentation was inadequate, what filled the gap, and what the documentation should now include.

---

## Three components of the structural shift.

**The orientation explicitly invites the documentation contribution.** The new hire knows from day one that your institution expects them to flag gaps as they encounter them.

**The onboarding cadence includes a structured 30-day, 60-day, and 90-day documentation review with the supervisor.** These are not performance reviews. They are documentation reviews.

**The improvements the new hire produces feed the function's annual refresh cycle.**

---

## What institutions that adopt this model report.

New hires reach independent productivity faster because the documentation environment is clearer. The portfolio improves at a higher cadence because the institution is capturing the freshest perspective on gaps with every staff transition.

---

## A question worth asking now.

What did your last three new hires produce in their first 90 days — and would any of it count as institutional documentation improvement?

The new hire's first ninety days are a learning window. The institutions that close the window without learning continue the cycle of documentation your institution thinks is adequate and that new hires find inadequate.
