Your institution is not short on org structure. There are reporting lines, cabinet hierarchies, and committee charters. The employee handbook publishes them. Every reorganization revises them.

What your institution usually doesn't have is a clear answer to one specific question. When a decision crosses departmental lines — financial aid plus IT, bursar plus registrar, accreditation plus general counsel — who owns it, in 60 seconds, without escalating to a cabinet meeting?

---

## RACI as decoration. RACI as architecture.

RACI matrices were invented to answer that question. In most institutions, they don't. The RACI lives in a slide deck from a 2019 reorganization. The actual routing happens through informal practice staff built because the formal chain was unclear or too slow. The cabinet absorbs the escalations because nobody else can.

This is not a paperwork gap. It is a decision architecture problem.

Decision architecture is the structural layer that determines how fast — and at what executive level — cross-departmental exceptions resolve. When it holds, ninety percent of cross-functional decisions resolve at the operational level. When it doesn't, the same decisions land on the president's desk.

---

## The 60-second test.

Pick a recent cross-functional exception. Ask three staff members: who owned the decision. If you get three different answers, the documented chain does not match the chain that actually ran.

The institutions whose answers converge in under sixty seconds have a load-bearing chain. The institutions whose answers diverge have decoration.

---

## Three authority gaps. Three fixes.

When the test produces divergent answers, the failure mode falls into one of three categories.

**Silent ownership.** The decision has a documented owner, but the owner doesn't know they own it. The RACI was published once and never socialized. The fix is socialization — publishing the chain, training the routing, monitoring the first six months.

**Bottleneck ownership.** The documented owner is also the documented owner of forty other decisions. The routing is technically correct but operationally unusable. The fix is delegation — pushing authority down to the operational level where the routing can actually scale.

**Ghost ownership.** The decision has no documented owner at all. The cross-functional case was not anticipated when the RACI was written. The fix is chain extension — adding the category and naming the owner.

---

## A question worth asking now.

RACI is not a document. It is the architecture that determines whether cross-departmental decisions resolve in sixty seconds or sixty days.

When a cross-functional exception hits your campus, do you have a 60-second answer — or a cabinet meeting?

Most institutions have a RACI. Most don't know whether it works until the moment it has to. The institutions that test it before the moment of truth are the institutions whose cabinets stop absorbing the cost of a chain that nobody published.
