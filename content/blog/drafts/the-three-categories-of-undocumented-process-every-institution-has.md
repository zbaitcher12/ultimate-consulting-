Your institution carries undocumented workflows. You know it. What most institutions don't carry is a taxonomy for what the undocumented work actually consists of.

The taxonomy matters because the three categories have different failure modes, different documentation paths, and different operational consequences.

---

## One generic approach doesn't work.

Treating undocumented operations as one problem produces generic SOP work nobody uses. Treating them as three distinct problems produces documentation that holds up.

The generic version is what most institutions have tried before. The binder lands. Staff don't use it because it doesn't address the gap they actually hit. The institution concludes that documentation work doesn't produce operational change — when the real conclusion is that generic documentation work doesn't produce operational change.

---

## Three categories. Three failure modes.

**Tribal knowledge.** Workflows your institution has run the same way for years without anyone writing them down. Fails on staff turnover. The documentation path is structured staff interviews aimed at workflow extraction — captured from the people who do the work.

**Configured-around exceptions.** Workarounds staff built because the system did not handle a real-world case. Fails on system migration. The documentation path is process-mapping that captures the official workflow and the actual workflow, side by side. The exceptions and handoffs make the record.

**Ghost approvals.** Decisions that get made without documented authority because the formal routing produced a delay that was operationally unacceptable. Fails on audit. The documentation path is authority-chain reconstruction that produces a RACI with the routing that actually runs.

---

## A question worth asking now.

The workflows that fail are the workflows your institution did not know it depended on.

Which of the three categories shows up most at your institution — and what would surfacing it cost the staff member who carries it today?

The taxonomy is the first step. The institutions that surface the inventory deliberately decide which categories to address before the failure mode does. The institutions that defer the inventory discover the categories at the moment of public consequence.
