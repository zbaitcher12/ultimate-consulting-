Higher education institutions do not operate under Sarbanes-Oxley. They operate under federal program requirements, state higher-education regulations, and audit standards from independent accountants and accreditors that have absorbed many SOX-style expectations without using the SOX language.

Your institution is expected to maintain internal controls that prevent fraud — dual approval on financial transactions above thresholds, segregation of duties between authorization and execution, documented audit trails for high-value workflows — but the controls are not always recognized as the documentation they actually are.

---

## Two portfolios that overlap and aren't maintained as one.

The institutions that have a clear internal-control framework tend to maintain it separately from their operational documentation portfolio. The internal-control framework lives in the controller's office or the audit committee charter. The operational documentation lives in the functional departments.

The two portfolios overlap substantially. They are not maintained as one.

---

## Where the overlap lives.

The dual-approval requirement on financial transactions above twenty-five thousand dollars is an internal control. It is also the procurement document, the contract execution document, and the disbursement authorization document.

The segregation-of-duties requirement between vendor setup and payment authorization is an internal control. It is also the accounts payable document and the vendor management document.

The audit-trail requirement on Title IV disbursements is an internal control. It is also the financial aid disbursement document.

---

## Three structural moves that integrate the portfolios.

**The controller's office participates in operational documentation governance** for the workflows that carry internal-control requirements.

**The operational documents include the internal-control language** — the specific control objective, the evidence requirement, the testing standard.

**The internal-control framework points to the operational documents** rather than maintaining parallel control documentation.

---

## What integration produces.

The institutions that have integrated these portfolios report that audit cycles are less disruptive, that internal control testing is faster because it maps to the document execution evidence, and that operational departments have a clearer view of which workflows carry internal-control requirements.

---

## A question worth asking now.

If you mapped your internal-control framework against your operational documentation, where would the gaps appear?

One set of documents. Two audiences. Half the maintenance cost. The institutions that recognize this build one portfolio that satisfies both purposes.
