The hardest SOPs to write in higher education are not the within-department workflows. Those have a clear owner and a clear scope. The hardest SOPs are the cross-functional handoffs — the workflows that require two departments to coordinate, that have ambiguous ownership, and that fail at the handoff point rather than inside either department.

---

## Three handoffs that show up as the most common failure points.

**Aid-to-bursar.** When financial aid disburses funds, how do the funds post to the student account, and what is the workflow when the posting fails or the timing is off.

**Registrar-to-IT.** When registration changes (drops, adds, withdrawals), how does the change propagate through the systems that depend on it, and what is the workflow when the propagation breaks.

**Advancement-to-finance.** When a gift is recorded, how does it flow through the financial systems, with donor-restriction tracking and fund accounting integrity.

---

## The documented version doesn't match the actual handoff.

The documented version typically describes a clean handoff in the happy path. The actual handoff includes the exceptions — the timing mismatches, the data quality issues, the manual interventions — that are necessary to keep the workflow moving but that live in no SOP.

---

## What cross-functional SOPs need to include.

**Joint ownership.** The SOP is owned by both departments, with named co-owners.

**Happy path plus exception paths.** The exceptions and handoffs the manual never captured, captured.

**Timing expectations at each handoff point.** The SOP names what the receiving department should expect and when.

**Escalation routing for when the handoff fails.** The SOP names who to contact when the timing slips or the data fails to arrive.

**Annual joint refresh.** The SOP is refreshed by both departments on a cadence that catches drift before it becomes dysfunction.

---

## A question worth asking now.

What's your most-broken cross-departmental handoff — and which SOP would close it?

The handoff is where your institution actually lives. The SOPs that document it move your institution from siloed function to coordinated work.
