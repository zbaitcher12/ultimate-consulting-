Your cabinet is full of people who carry decision authority. The org chart says so. Position descriptions say so. The RACI from the last reorganization says so.

What is harder to confirm is whether the documented chain matches the way decisions actually route when an exception hits.

---

## A one-hour diagnostic.

Pick a recent cross-functional exception — something that crossed departmental lines and required a decision in under a week. Ask three staff members at three different levels: who owned the decision. Then ask the documented owner: did you know you owned it.

If the answers converge in under sixty seconds, the documented chain is load-bearing. If they diverge, the documented chain is decoration, and the actual chain is being reconstructed informally every time an exception hits.

The test is uncomfortable. Results are almost always more degraded than cabinet expects. The documented chain says one thing; staff practice says another; the gap is rarely small.

---

## Three failure modes. Three fixes.

**Silent ownership.** The documented owner has the authority but doesn't know it. The decision routes around them. The fix is socialization.

**Bottleneck ownership.** The documented owner has the authority but cannot process the volume. The routing collapses to whoever can. The fix is delegation — pushing authority to the operational level.

**Ghost ownership.** No one has documented authority for this category of decision, because the category was not anticipated. The routing is invented. The fix is chain extension.

---

## This is not a failure of the staff doing the routing.

The staff routing decisions informally are doing what they have to do to keep the institution running. The undocumented routing is what emerged in the absence of a documented chain that actually worked.

---

## Sixty seconds is the threshold.

Sixty seconds is not arbitrary. It's the threshold above which staff who need a decision start to route around the chain. Below sixty seconds, the chain works. Above sixty seconds, the chain is decoration.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Can your team run the 60-second test on your three highest-frequency cross-functional decisions this week?

The institutions that run the diagnostic deliberately restore the chain to load-bearing. The institutions that defer the diagnostic continue to absorb the cost of a chain that exists only in slide decks.
