Most documentation projects in higher education fail. The failure mode is not always visible immediately. The work produces a finished document. The document looks like an SOP. The failure becomes visible six months later, when nobody is using it, the workflow has drifted further from the documented version, and the next operational crisis exposes the gap.

The failure is rarely a delivery problem. The work produced what was asked for. The failure is an upstream-decision problem.

---

## Five decisions made before kickoff predict the outcome.

**Scope.** Work that succeeds is scoped to a specific workflow, a specific compliance gap, or a specific cross-functional handoff. Work that fails is scoped to a function, a department, or your institution. The scope at the right level produces a document specific enough to land. The scope at the wrong level produces a document too general to use.

**Sponsor.** Work that succeeds has one named executive sponsor with cabinet alignment. Work that fails has presidential sponsorship without a defined executive owner, cabinet sponsorship without a single accountable owner, or no executive sponsor at all. The single-accountable-owner structure produces decisions when decisions are needed.

**Sequencing.** Work that succeeds starts with the highest-leverage workflow — the one where documentation gaps are producing the most cost, the highest risk, or the most cross-functional friction. Work that fails starts with whichever workflow the loudest staff member or the most engaged supervisor wants to start with.

**Governance.** Work that succeeds defines, before kickoff, who owns the document after delivery, who refreshes it on what cadence, and what the change-control workflow looks like. Work that fails leaves these questions for after the document lands. By the time the questions are asked, the work is over.

**Post-handoff ownership.** Work that succeeds includes, in scope, the transition to internal ownership. The last document is the handoff to the named internal owner. Work that fails ends at document handoff with no explicit transition.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Most documentation projects fail. The five decisions are how to be the exception.

Which of the five decisions has your institution made deliberately — and which has been left to drift?

The institutions that run the diagnostic produce work that compounds. The institutions that skip it produce binders that decay.
