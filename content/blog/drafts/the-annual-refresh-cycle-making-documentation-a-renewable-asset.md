Documentation does not refresh itself. The workflow the document describes keeps evolving — staff change, systems update, regulations shift, exceptions accumulate — and the document that was accurate at publication becomes inaccurate over time. The institutions that treat documents as one-time outputs produce documents that decay. The institutions that treat them as renewable assets produce documents that compound.

---

## Three components of the annual refresh.

**Trigger events that start a refresh.** Calendar triggers (reviewed annually on a scheduled date). Change triggers (a relevant system, regulation, or staff change). Audit triggers (a finding or program review surfaces a gap). Institutions that use only one trigger type miss the drift the others catch. Institutions that use all three catch drift consistently.

**The distinction between lightweight refresh and full rewrite.** Most refreshes do not require a full rewrite. The lightweight refresh confirms the workflow has not changed materially, updates the version date, and logs the review. The full rewrite is reserved for refreshes that surface material changes — new workflow steps, new exceptions, new routing, new evidence requirements.

**Version control and change-log discipline.** The current document is identifiable. Prior versions are retrievable. The change log specifies what changed, when, and why.

---

## Why the distinction matters operationally.

The distinction between lightweight refresh and full rewrite is what makes the annual cycle sustainable. The institutions that treat every refresh as a full rewrite consume more bandwidth than your institution can sustain. The institutions that distinguish run the cycle at sustainable cost.

Version control matters because auditors and program reviewers occasionally ask for documentation as it existed at the time of a specific transaction or finding. The institutions with disciplined version control produce the historical record. The institutions without, cannot.

---

## A question worth asking now.

When was your highest-volume Title IV procedure last refreshed — and what's triggered the next refresh date?

Documentation that does not refresh becomes decoration. The cadence is what keeps it load-bearing.
