The document with no internal owner becomes the document nobody updates. The document nobody updates drifts from operational reality within twelve months. The drifted document either keeps getting referenced as if current, producing operational inconsistency, or gets recognized as stale and ignored, producing the documentation gap the work was commissioned to close.

Both outcomes return your institution to the pre-work state.

---

## Governance is the structural prevention.

The institution that names an owner, defines a refresh cadence, and builds a change-control workflow before the work closes is the institution whose documentation persists as a load-bearing asset.

The institution that defers governance until after the finished document lands is the institution whose documentation decays.

---

## Three governance models work in higher education.

**Process owner.** Each document has a named individual owner with refresh authority. The owner is typically the supervisor of the function the document covers. Works when the function has a stable supervisor with bandwidth to own the refresh work. Fails when the supervisor turns over frequently or when bandwidth is consumed by operations.

**Governance committee.** A standing committee owns the portfolio collectively, with rotating chair responsibility and a published refresh schedule. Works when your institution has the cross-functional alignment to maintain the committee. Fails when the committee becomes the place where documentation work goes to die because no individual owns the refresh output.

**Embedded ops.** The portfolio is owned by a small operational team — typically one to three staff — whose function is to maintain the portfolio across all departments. Works when your institution has the staffing depth to dedicate the positions. Fails when it does not.

---

## Most institutions choose between two of the three.

At concentrated-staffing institutions, the embedded-ops model is often the wrong fit — the staffing is calibrated for throughput rather than for a dedicated ops function. The right choice is typically between process owner and governance committee, both of which work well at that staffing density.

Process owner works for institutions with stable functional leadership. Governance committee works for institutions with cross-functional alignment but inconsistent functional tenure.

The decision is institution-specific.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Six months after your last documentation work closed, who at your institution owns the procedures — and how often do they get refreshed?

The institutions that decide before the work closes build portfolios. The institutions that decide later, or not at all, rebuild every two years.
