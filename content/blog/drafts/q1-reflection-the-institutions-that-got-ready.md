The first quarter of an operating-model push reveals patterns the rest of the year refines. Institutions that exit Q1 with a clearer view of how the work actually runs are the institutions that have done three specific things. Institutions that exit Q1 with the same view as January 1 are usually the institutions that did one or two of the three — or did all three at a resource commitment that did not match the work.

---

## Three patterns. Not proportional to budget.

**Naming.** Institutions that made progress named the challenge in language specific enough to act on. Generic naming — operating discipline, process maturity, institutional readiness — produces conversation. Specific naming — Title IV documentation maturity in the aid office, decision-authority clarity for cross-departmental exceptions, audit-readiness posture for the next SACSCOC review cycle — produces work.

**Sponsorship.** Institutions that made progress had a named executive sponsor with a defined scope and a defined timeline. Sponsorship from the president alone tended to lose momentum when the president's calendar shifted. Sponsorship from the cabinet collectively tended to lose accountability. Sponsorship from one named executive — usually the CFO, sometimes the CIO, occasionally the chief of staff — with the cabinet aligned behind them tended to hold.

**Cadence.** Institutions that made progress ran the work on a cadence that produced visible finished documents every four to six weeks. The documents were not always large. What mattered was that they were visible, named, and consumed by the cabinet. Cadence kept the work in the operating cycle rather than letting it drift into the strategic-initiative category that gets revisited annually.

---

## Why the institutions that stalled stalled.

The institutions that exited Q1 with the same view as January 1 typically had at least one of these patterns broken. They named the work generically and did not act on it. They had presidential sponsorship without a defined executive owner. They committed to the work without a cadence, so it drifted into the open-ended-initiative category nobody is responsible for closing.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Did your institution exit Q1 with a clearer view of how the work actually runs — or the same view as January 1?

The quarter that compounds is the quarter that names, sponsors, and runs the cadence. The retrospective is how your institution finds out which of the three it did well and which it did not.
