Your CIO operates with dashboards that measure system uptime, ticket volume, security posture, and project status. Each reflects metrics the CIO has been tracking for years. What CIOs do not typically have is a view of how system configurations align with documented operational workflows.

---

## The question system uptime doesn't answer.

Configuration-to-workflow alignment is the most important measure your CIO is not tracking. It addresses the question that produces the most expensive IT failures: do the system configurations match how operations actually run, or do they match how someone assumed operations would run when the system was implemented.

---

## Three measures that produce a useful view.

**Configuration audit pass rate.** For your institution's most operationally important configurations — financial aid processing rules, student record routing, payment posting logic — what percentage pass an audit against documented operational workflow. The pass rate is rarely a hundred percent.

**Change-management adherence.** For system changes implemented in the last twelve months, what percentage followed the documented change-management workflow with completed authorization, testing, and rollback documentation. Indicates whether the IT shop is adding to or reducing configuration debt with each change.

**Document-to-system-of-record traceability.** For your institution's operational documents, what percentage have an identified system of record and an identified configuration trace to it. Indicates whether the documentation portfolio and the system landscape are managed as one operational reality or as two parallel tracks.

---

## A question worth asking now.

What percentage of your current configuration decisions can be traced back to a documented workflow?

System uptime is a baseline. Configuration-to-workflow alignment is the strategic measure. The CIOs who track both have institutional visibility their peers do not.
