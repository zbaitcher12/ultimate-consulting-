Marketing departments in higher education love campaigns and resist documentation. The combination produces brand consistency that depends entirely on the institutional memory of the staff in the seats. When the marketing team turns over — and higher-ed marketing turnover is elevated relative to other institutional functions — the brand consistency goes with the team.

---

## A cycle reliable enough to predict.

A new chief marketing officer arrives. She sees a brand expression that is inconsistent across channels, audiences, and applications. She commissions a brand refresh. The refresh produces a new standard. The new standard lasts approximately the tenure of the CMO. The next CMO arrives and starts the cycle again.

The institutions that break the cycle are the institutions that have captured the brand standard at a level of operational detail that survives staff transitions.

---

## Three marketing workflows consistently underdocumented.

**Campaign brief.** The workflow that translates a strategic objective into a campaign specification, with the brand parameters, the channel selection, the budget alignment, and the success metrics.

**Approval chain.** The routing that authorizes campaign content before publication, with the brand review, the executive review, and the legal review where applicable.

**Asset library governance.** The workflow that maintains the brand asset library — logos, color palettes, fonts, photography, templates — and the access rules that govern who can use which assets and how.

---

## How each failure compounds.

Campaign briefs fail when each staff member uses a different brief template, producing inconsistent strategic alignment. Approval chains fail when the routing is unclear and content publishes without brand review, producing the inconsistencies that drive the next CMO to commission another brand refresh. Asset library governance fails when staff use stale logos, off-brand colors, or unapproved photography because the current assets were not findable.

The SOPs that close these gaps are not lengthy. The campaign brief SOP is a template plus the routing for completion. The approval chain SOP is a one-page workflow that names the reviewers and the timing. The asset library governance SOP is the access policy plus the refresh cadence.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Could your institution's brand consistency survive a full marketing team turnover this fiscal year?

Marketing teams love campaigns. The campaigns produce the visible work. The SOPs produce the brand that survives the team's transition. The institutions that build both produce brand equity that compounds across years. The institutions that build only the campaigns produce brand equity that resets.
