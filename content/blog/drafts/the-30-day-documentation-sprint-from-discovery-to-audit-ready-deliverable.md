Documentation work that takes six months to produce a finished document is the work that loses institutional momentum. The cabinet that funded it in January is not the same cabinet that sees the document in July. The president who sponsored it has moved to the next strategic priority. The staff who sat through the first conversation have been pulled into the next crisis.

The document lands in a context where nobody remembers why it was commissioned.

---

## A different structure, not a compression.

The institutions that get value from documentation work run it on a thirty-day cadence. First conversation to first audit-ready document in four weeks. The cabinet that funded it remembers funding it. The staff who sat through the first week see their input in the write-up. The momentum carries into the next cycle.

Thirty days is not a compression of a six-month project. It is a different structure.

The scope is narrow — one cross-functional workflow, one functional area, one specific compliance requirement. The participants are tightly defined — the staff who execute the workflow, the supervisor accountable for the outcome, the audit liaison who owns the evidence trail. The write-up is calibrated to thirty days.

---

## The week-by-week cadence.

**Week one. Capture.** Three to five staff interviews with the people who execute the workflow. Surface the documented version, the actual version, the exceptions, the workarounds, the dependencies.

**Week two. Draft.** A first draft that incorporates staff input, with exceptions named and handoffs documented.

**Week three. Review.** The draft circulates to the staff who provided input, the supervisor accountable for the outcome, and the audit liaison. Review surfaces gaps, inconsistencies, remaining questions.

**Week four. Ship.** The refined write-up is published, the named owner is identified, the first onboarding test is scheduled.

---

## What the cadence demands from your institution.

The thirty-day cadence is more demanding than the six-month cadence. Staff who execute the workflow have to make time for three to five interview hours in week one. The supervisor makes time for review in week three. The audit liaison makes time for evidence-trail validation. The cabinet makes time for the review at the end of week four.

What your institution gets in exchange is a document that lands while the context is still live.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Thirty days is the window inside which institutional context stays live.

If your institution committed to a 30-day cycle on its highest-risk workflow, what would the finished document look like?

The work that fits inside the window produces documents your institution uses. The work that doesn't fit inside the window produces binders your institution files.
