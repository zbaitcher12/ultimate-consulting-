Retention dollars in higher education are usually framed as program dollars. New advising structures. Supplemental instruction. Early-alert systems. Care teams.

The framing misses where retention work actually fails.

---

## Retention work fails in the spaces between the programs.

Retention work fails in the case management workflow that runs between the program touchpoints. When a student appears on an early-alert dashboard, what happens. When the appearance becomes a follow-up call, what is the script. When the call becomes a referral to financial aid, what is the routing. When the referral resolves or stalls, what is on paper.

Most institutions do not have SOPs for these workflows. They have program structures. The structures fund the people who do the work. The workflows the people run are improvised based on individual judgment, which means the student experience is uneven by staff member, by case complexity, and by time of day.

---

## Four case management workflows that distinguish high-retention institutions.

**Triage.** When a flag appears, what determines whether the case is severity-one, severity-two, or routine.

**Referral.** What is the routing when the case requires intervention from another office, and what is the timing expectation.

**Follow-up.** What is the contact cadence with the student, and what triggers the next escalation.

**Outcome documentation.** When the case closes, what is captured in the record, and how does the record feed the institutional learning about which interventions work.

---

## How each one fails without paper.

Triage fails when the same flag produces different responses from different staff. Referral fails when the receiving office does not know what to do with the handoff. Follow-up fails when the student stops responding and nobody is sure whether to keep calling. Outcome documentation fails when the case closes without your institution learning whether the intervention worked.

The cost is the retention loss your institution funded the program to prevent. The student who appeared on the dashboard, who received a call from one staff member but not another, who was referred but never followed up with, who stopped responding without anyone deciding what to do — that student does not return for the next term.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Where in your student services case-management workflow does the SOP run out — and what happens to the student then?

Retention dollars do not produce retention. Retention work does. The work runs on case management SOPs that distinguish what happens consistently from what happens by chance.
