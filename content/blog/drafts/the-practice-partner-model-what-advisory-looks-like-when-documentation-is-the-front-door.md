The outside-help industry in higher education has a structural incentive problem. Firms earn revenue by being needed. The longer the client needs the firm, the more revenue the firm earns. This produces work structures that, even with the best intentions, tend toward ongoing dependency rather than durable internal capability.

---

## The structural alternative.

A relationship designed for capability transfer. The work starts with a defined document. The document produces operational change. The change is owned internally before the work closes. The next piece of work, if there is one, addresses a different problem rather than maintaining the work of the prior piece.

---

## What documentation-as-front-door looks like.

When this alternative uses documentation work as the front door, the model becomes specific.

The first piece of work is a documentation piece on the highest-priority workflow. The finished output is the document, the named internal owner, the governance model, and the refresh cadence. The work closes with the internal team owning what was captured.

If a second piece of work follows, it addresses a different workflow with the same structure. Your institution accumulates documents and internal capability across multiple pieces of work rather than ongoing retainer dependence on one firm.

---

## Three differences from transactional outside help.

**Scope structure.** Transactional help scopes work around projects with extended timelines and broad outputs. Capability-transfer work scopes around specific documents on short cycles. The transactional piece is six months. The capability-transfer piece is two to four weeks per document.

**Handoff structure.** Transactional help hands off the finished work to your institution and may or may not include a transition to internal ownership. Capability-transfer work includes the transition to internal ownership as the closing document.

**Economic structure.** Transactional help prices work at a level that supports ongoing firm involvement. Capability-transfer work prices at a level your institution can sustain across multiple iterations.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Has your institution had an outside relationship that built durable internal capability — or always rebuilt it from scratch with the next firm?

The relationship that builds capability looks different from the one that extends dependency. The structural differences show up in the scope, the handoff design, and the economics.
