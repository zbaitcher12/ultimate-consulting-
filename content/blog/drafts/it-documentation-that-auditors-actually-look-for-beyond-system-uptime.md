IT departments in higher education typically have SOPs. The SOPs are usually the wrong ones. The documented procedures cover routine tasks staff already know how to run. The gaps cover the high-stakes scenarios auditors and federal reviewers now expect to see written down at audit-grade quality.

---

## Five workflows that moved from optional to required.

Federal expectations and audit standards have shifted. Five IT workflows are now expected to be written down at audit-grade quality.

**Incident response.** The runbook staff follow when a security incident occurs, with the timing requirements, the notification routing, the evidence preservation, and the post-incident review.

**Change management.** The workflow that authorizes and tracks system changes, with the approval routing, the testing requirements, and the rollback procedures.

**Access provisioning and deprovisioning.** The workflow that grants and revokes system access, with role-based authorization and timely revocation on staff departure.

**Data classification and handling.** The framework that distinguishes which data carries which protection requirements, with the workflows that hold the distinctions.

**Backup and recovery.** The procedures that protect against data loss, with the testing cadence that confirms recovery actually works.

---

## How each one fails when undocumented.

Incident response fails at two a.m. when the on-call staff member cannot find the runbook, contacts the wrong people in the wrong order, and preserves evidence inconsistently. Change management fails when an unauthorized change produces a production outage and the rollback procedure does not exist. Access provisioning fails when terminated staff retain system access for weeks. Data classification fails when staff handle protected data without recognizing the protection requirements. Backup and recovery fails when your institution discovers, during a ransomware incident, that the backups have not been tested in eighteen months and are corrupt.

---

## What audit-grade documentation looks like.

The SOPs audit-grade IT operations require are operational, current, and tested. The incident response runbook lives in a location on-call staff can reach without authenticated access. Change management integrates with the ticket system that produces the audit trail. Access provisioning runs from the HR system that authorizes the role, with automated deprovisioning on termination.

---

## A question worth asking now.

When was your incident response runbook last tested — and would your on-call staff find it if they needed it at 2 a.m.?

The IT SOPs that exist are usually the wrong ones. The audit checks for the right ones. The institutions that build the right SOPs before the audit asks are the institutions that exit reviews cleanly.
