Federal program reviews aren't primarily designed to catch institutions doing something wrong. They're designed to verify that your institution can show, in documented evidence, that an established workflow was followed.

That single distinction reframes everything about audit readiness.

---

## Audit readiness is a workflow problem. Not a paperwork backlog.

The most common finding in federal program reviews is not fraud. It is not intentional non-compliance. It is the inability to show that a workflow was followed — because it wasn't written down, or the write-up wasn't maintained, or the evidence trail wasn't preserved.

The institutions that exit reviews cleanly are not the institutions that wrote a binder the week before the review. They are the institutions where the way the work actually runs produces documented evidence as a byproduct.

The audit is not the problem. The way your institution operates is what the audit is measuring.

---

## Three categories. Three remediation paths.

**The documentation gap.** The workflow exists. Staff follow it. Nobody wrote it down. The fix is structured documentation work folded into the operating cadence, not triggered by the next review.

**The consistency gap.** The workflow is written down, but staff execute it inconsistently because the write-up does not match operational reality. The fix is rewriting the workflow by interviewing the staff who actually execute it, not the supervisors who think they know how it runs.

**The evidence gap.** The workflow is written down and executed consistently, but the audit trail does not survive the review window. The fix is building the trail into the workflow itself — the act of running the work generates the evidence.

---

## Six weeks. Three workflows. Close the loop.

Six weeks. Your three highest-risk workflows. Document, refine, close the evidence loop. That's what it takes to produce the way the work runs — on paper, with a trail — that survives the next review without prep, and the one after that.

---

## A question worth asking now.

If a federal reviewer walked into your institution tomorrow, could your staff show documented, consistent evidence of how your highest-risk workflows actually run?

The audit that didn't find fraud — it found no process. The work that prevents the finding is the work the institution should have been doing anyway. Runs when people leave. Holds up when someone asks "says who?"
