Gap analysis in higher education has a structural failure mode that recurs regardless of advisor, methodology, or institutional context. The analysis takes six months. The findings arrive in a context where the operating cycle has moved on. Your institution files the findings and continues operating with the gaps the analysis surfaced.

---

## What working speed looks like.

The institutions that get value from gap analysis run it at working speed. Two-week cycles. Narrow scope. Specific findings that map to specific decisions. The findings arrive while your cabinet still remembers commissioning the work, and your institution acts on them inside the same operating quarter.

---

## Three categories of finding that surface.

When gap analysis runs at working speed, findings fall into three categories.

**Workflow gaps.** The documented workflow does not match operational reality, and the gap shows up as inconsistency in execution. Fix: SOP revision that aligns the write-up with how the work actually runs.

**Authority gaps.** The workflow has unclear decision routing, and the gap shows up as queue delays at decision points. Fix: RACI extension that names the missing decision owners.

**Measurement gaps.** The workflow runs, the authority is clear, and your institution has no instrumentation to know whether the workflow is producing the intended outcome. Fix: metric instrumentation that builds the dashboard or report layer the workflow needs.

---

## What distinguishes fast from slow.

**Scope.** Working-speed analysis covers one workflow at a time, fully, rather than your institution comprehensively, partially.

**Participation.** Working-speed analysis goes deep with the staff who do the work, rather than broad with the supervisors who think they know how the workflow runs.

**Output.** Working-speed analysis produces a decision matrix — which findings to close in the next ninety days, which to close in the next operating year, which to accept as inherited risk — rather than a comprehensive list of gaps with no action attached.

---

## Why the old pattern is hard to defend.

The institutions that have lived through extended-timeline gap analysis are usually the institutions most receptive to working-speed analysis, because they have seen the failure mode. The findings that arrived six months late are the findings that nobody acted on.

---

## A question worth asking now.

If you ran gap analysis on your three highest-Title IV-volume workflows this week, what would it surface?

The document nobody uses is the document that arrived too late. The document that lands is the one that arrived while the operating cycle still had room to act on it.
