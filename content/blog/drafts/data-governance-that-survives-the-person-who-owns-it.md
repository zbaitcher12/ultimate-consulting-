Your institutional research function runs on one or two staff members carrying the entire reporting load. IPEDS submissions. Accreditation evidence. Board dashboards. Strategic-plan metrics. The long tail of ad hoc requests from cabinet members who need a number for a meeting on Thursday.

---

## Concentrated by design. Risk by default.

The function is concentrated by design — institutions calibrate staffing for throughput, not redundancy — and the concentration becomes a problem when the IR director takes a position at another institution, retires, or is unavailable during a reporting deadline.

Your institution discovers that the data definitions, the refresh cadence, the dashboard logic, and the FERPA boundaries were carried in one staff member's memory.

---

## Four gaps that produce the highest transition risk.

The SOPs that solve this are lightweight, operational, and focused on four specific gaps.

**Data definitions.** What does your institution count as an enrolled student. What counts as a credit hour. What counts as a degree completion. Definitions vary by reporting context — IPEDS uses one definition, the state uses another, the strategic plan uses a third — and your IR director carries the mapping.

**Refresh cadence.** When does each data source refresh, and when does the refresh feed the downstream report. The CFO who runs the Tuesday cabinet meeting from the enrollment dashboard needs to know whether the number reflects last Friday's snapshot or this morning's load.

**Dashboard governance.** Who is authorized to modify dashboards. Who reviews changes before publication. What is the testing standard.

**FERPA boundaries.** What data can be shared with which audiences, in what aggregation. The cabinet member who asks for a list of at-risk students by name needs to be routed differently than the one who asks for the count of at-risk students by program.

---

## A question worth asking now.

How many of your institution's reporting outputs depend on one IR staff member's memory?

Most IR functions are one staff member deep. The SOPs are what make that survivable. The institutions that build them are the institutions that don't lose six months of reporting capacity to a single resignation.
