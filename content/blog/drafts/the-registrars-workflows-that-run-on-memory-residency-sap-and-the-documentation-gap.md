The registrar's office runs more workflows on institutional memory than any other function in higher education. Residency classification is the most extreme example, but the pattern is consistent across the office. Workflows that require judgment. Workflows that carry compliance consequences. Workflows that have been refined over years by staff who never wrote the refinements down.

---

## Four workflows that show up consistently underdocumented.

**Residency classification.** The logic that determines in-state versus out-of-state tuition status, with state-specific rules that vary by domicile, dependency, military status, employer relationship, and a long tail of exceptions.

**Satisfactory academic progress.** The workflow that determines whether a student remains in good academic standing for Title IV eligibility, with appeals and probationary structures.

**Transfer credit evaluation.** The rules that determine how external credit applies to internal program requirements, often with negotiated articulation agreements that live as side documents.

**FERPA response.** The routing for student record requests, with consent verification and disclosure logging.

---

## Each one fails predictably on staff transition.

Your residency deputy transfers to another institution. Your SAP coordinator goes on medical leave. Your transfer evaluator retires. Your FERPA-trained staff member is reassigned during a system migration. In each case, your institution discovers within thirty days that the workflow was running on the person, not on paper.

The cost is rarely absorbed at the operational level. Residency errors produce tuition appeals that escalate to cabinet. SAP errors produce Title IV findings that produce regulatory exposure. Transfer credit errors produce academic-progression complications that produce student complaints. FERPA errors produce legal exposure.

---

## The SOPs that close these gaps are not generic.

Residency SOPs are state-specific — the logic for Texas does not map cleanly to Florida. SAP SOPs are institution-specific — the appeal process and probationary structure reflect policy decisions that vary by mission. Transfer credit SOPs are program-specific. FERPA SOPs are partially federal and partially institutional. All of them have to be captured from the people who do the work, exceptions and handoffs included.

---

## A question worth asking now.

If your residency deputy left tomorrow, how long until the next exception became a tuition appeal?

The registrar's office is the function where workflow-on-memory has the highest probability of producing a public consequence. The SOPs do not eliminate the risk. They reduce the probability that the staff transition becomes the public consequence.
