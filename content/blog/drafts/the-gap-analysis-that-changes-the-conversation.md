Gap analysis is one of the most overused terms and most underutilized tools in higher education. It shows up on project charters, accreditation responses, vendor proposals, and reports. Most of the time it produces a generic document that names obvious gaps in obvious terms and surfaces nothing actionable.

---

## What actionable gap analysis does differently.

The institutions that get value from gap analysis are running a different version. The difference shows up in three specific places.

**Scope.** Generic gap analysis is institution-wide. Actionable gap analysis is workflow-specific — one cross-functional workflow at a time, named in operational terms.

**Participants.** Generic gap analysis goes to supervisors. Actionable gap analysis goes to the staff who execute the workflow, because they are the only people who know the difference between what your institution believes it is running and what it is actually running.

**Output.** Generic gap analysis produces a list with no action attached. Actionable gap analysis produces a finding-by-finding decision matrix: which gaps to close in the next ninety days, which to close in the next operating year, which to accept as inherited risk.

---

## Three categories of finding.

When the analysis is properly scoped, findings fall into three categories.

**Workflow gaps.** The workflow exists, has documented owners, and is not being followed because the documented version does not match operational reality. The fix is SOP revision — captured from the people who do the work.

**Authority gaps.** The workflow exists, is being followed, and decision authority is unclear, so the workflow stalls at the first exception. The fix is RACI extension.

**Measurement gaps.** The workflow exists, is being followed, has clear authority, and your institution has no way to know whether it is working. The fix is metric instrumentation.

---

## Narrow scope produces decisions. Broad scope produces reports.

The narrow scope produces decisions. The broad scope produces reports.

The institutions that get value from gap analysis are the ones whose analysis fits inside the operating cadence rather than displacing it.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Has gap analysis at your institution produced a decision — or just another report?

The report nobody uses is the gap analysis that named everything in general and nothing in particular. The one that changes the conversation is the one that produced a decision your cabinet was not having before.
