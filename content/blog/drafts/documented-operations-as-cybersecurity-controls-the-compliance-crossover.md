Cybersecurity controls in higher education moved from optional to required in the last five years. Federal expectations around data protection, breach notification, and incident response have tightened. Cyber insurance underwriters now expect documented controls as a precondition for coverage. State data protection regulations have proliferated.

The institutions that didn't have documented cybersecurity controls in 2020 are the institutions that have spent the last three years building them.

---

## What most institutions discover during the build.

The cybersecurity controls being asked for are operational workflows your institution already runs — change management, access provisioning, incident response, data handling — just captured at a level of detail the IT operation had not previously formalized.

The cybersecurity control build is largely a documentation exercise applied to IT operations.

---

## Capture once. Comply twice.

The documents that describe IT operations are the same documents that satisfy cybersecurity audits. Four IT workflows do most of the work.

**Change management.** The workflow that authorizes and tracks system changes. The document that describes it for operational consistency is the same document that satisfies the cybersecurity control requirement that changes be authorized, tested, and rolled back when necessary.

**Access provisioning and deprovisioning.** The workflow that grants and revokes system access. The document satisfies the cybersecurity control requirement that access be granted on a least-privilege basis and revoked timely on staff departure.

**Incident response.** The runbook staff follow when a security incident occurs. The document satisfies the cybersecurity control requirement that incidents be detected, escalated, contained, and reported on documented timelines.

**Data handling.** The workflow that classifies data and specifies handling requirements by classification. The document satisfies the cybersecurity control requirement that data be classified and handled according to classification.

---

## What integration requires.

The cybersecurity officer participates in operational documentation governance. Operational documents include the cybersecurity control requirements they address. The audit-readiness pipeline and the cybersecurity controls portfolio run as one portfolio.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Which of your operational documents would also satisfy a cybersecurity control audit — and which need a 20% refresh to qualify?

The cybersecurity controls and the IT operational documents are the same documents, read by different audiences. The institutions that build them as one portfolio are the ones that comply efficiently.
