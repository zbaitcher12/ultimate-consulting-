Higher-education boards measure what matters to institutional survival. Enrollment. Financial position. Fundraising performance. Academic outcomes.

Each appears on the board scorecard in standardized form. Each gets discussed at every meeting.

---

## What doesn't appear on the scorecard.

The record of how the work actually runs. Boards hear about it when something has gone wrong — an audit finding, a system failure, a compliance issue, a personnel matter that revealed a gap.

Boards don't hear about it as a forward-looking indicator that distinguishes institutions that will continue running well from institutions that are accumulating risk that will eventually surface.

This is a measurement gap, not a strategic gap. The reasons the record matters at the operational level — turnover absorption, audit posture, cutover readiness, decision velocity — matter at the board level too. The board that monitors enrollment without monitoring the record is monitoring the symptom without monitoring the leading indicator.

---

## Three measurable indicators that translate.

**Documentation portfolio coverage.** What percentage of your institution's load-bearing workflows have current, documented procedures with named owners. The trend matters more than the absolute. An institution at sixty percent trending up over four quarters is in a healthier position than an institution at seventy percent trending down.

**Refresh currency.** What percentage of the portfolio has been refreshed within its scheduled cadence. An institution with a structured refresh cycle producing eighty-five percent currency is in a healthier position than one with sporadic refresh producing ninety-five percent through a one-time cleanup.

**Cross-functional handoff documentation.** How many of your institution's named cross-functional workflows have documented handoff procedures with joint ownership. An institution growing from three documented handoffs to nine over four quarters is in a healthier position than one sitting at twelve undocumented handoffs.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Does your board hear quarterly numbers on how the work actually runs — or only when something has gone wrong?

Boards measure what matters. The record of how the work runs matters. The institutions that put it on the scorecard elevate the conversation.
