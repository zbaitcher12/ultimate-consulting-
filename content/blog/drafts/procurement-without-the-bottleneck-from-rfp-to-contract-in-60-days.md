Procurement is the function where SOP gaps compound. It sits between operations and finance. Approval routing crosses departmental lines. Decision authority often lives in committees rather than individuals.

The result is that a two-week RFP delay in any single step compounds to a six-month total cycle, and your institution discovers it cannot move on the underlying strategic initiative because the procurement is stuck.

---

## Institutions that close RFPs in sixty days.

The institutions that close RFPs in sixty days are not the ones with better procurement staff. They are the ones with documented procurement SOPs that name the routing, the decision points, the timing expectations, and the escalation paths.

Four procurement workflows show up undocumented at most institutions.

**RFP scoping.** The workflow that translates a strategic need into an RFP specification, with the operational input, the technical input, and the budgetary alignment.

**RFP evaluation.** The workflow that scores proposals, with the rubric, the evaluation committee composition, and the scoring transparency.

**Contract negotiation.** The workflow that moves from selected vendor to signed contract, with the legal review, the term negotiation, and the signature routing.

**Contract execution.** The workflow that operationalizes the signed contract, with vendor onboarding, access provisioning, and relationship management.

---

## Timing benchmarks that distinguish operating mode.

Each workflow has timing expectations the institutions in operating mode meet and the institutions in event mode miss. Scoping: two to four weeks. Evaluation: three to six weeks. Negotiation: two to four weeks. Execution: two to four weeks.

The institutions that hit these benchmarks have documented SOPs. The ones that miss them rebuild the routing every RFP because the routing was not documented from the previous one.

---

## The hidden cost procurement doesn't see.

The cost of procurement delay is rarely captured as a procurement cost. It is captured as the strategic initiative that did not launch on schedule, the donor relationship that did not close, the program that did not start in the fall term because the technology was not ready. Your CFO sees the cost as a missed budget projection. Your procurement office sees only the work it processed. The connection between the delay and the cost is not made.

---

## A question worth asking now.

What's the longest open RFP at your institution right now — and which SOP gap is it stuck on?

The procurement RFP that takes six months is the procurement RFP stuck on undocumented routing. The acceleration is structural, not heroic.
