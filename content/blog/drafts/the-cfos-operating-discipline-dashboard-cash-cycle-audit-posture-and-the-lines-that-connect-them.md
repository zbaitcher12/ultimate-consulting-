Your CFO already operates with dashboards that measure cash position, audit posture, and budget variance. Each dashboard reflects metrics the CFO has been tracking for years. What CFOs do not typically have is a view of how their existing metrics are downstream of gaps in the record of how the work runs.

---

## Three categories directly downstream.

The connection is not abstract. It is mechanical.

**Cash-application aging.** The cash that arrives at your institution but does not post to student accounts within a defined window is cash that produces operational friction — student inquiries, account holds, retention risk, eventual collections. The aging is partly a function of payment-source variability and partly a function of the bursar's exception-handling discipline. The metric is downstream of bursar documentation.

**Audit finding-to-close cycle.** The time between an audit finding and the documented closure is a CFO metric that reflects remediation capacity. The metric is downstream of your institution's documentation refresh and remediation capability.

**Exception-rate trends.** The rate at which institutional workflows produce exceptions that require manual intervention is a metric most CFOs track at the function level. The metric is downstream of the documentation that defines the workflow and the exception handling.

---

## Why CFOs who see the connection intervene differently.

The CFOs who recognize the connection intervene at the documentation level rather than at the metric level. Intervening at the metric level produces incremental improvement. Intervening at the upstream documentation level produces structural improvement that compounds.

---

## A question worth asking now.

Which of your CFO's existing dashboard metrics is actually downstream of a documentation gap?

CFOs already measure cash and audit. The record of how the work runs is the leading indicator behind both. The CFOs who see the connection make the upstream investments. The CFOs who don't spend on the symptoms.
