The system goes live on schedule. The cutover is clean. The status report turns green. Three weeks in, your aid specialist is still running the old spreadsheet because the new workflow configures around a process that only lived in her head.

The system is live. The institution isn't.

---

## Two different readinesses. One got budgeted. One didn't.

Technical readiness has a defined scope, a defined timeline, and a deliverable that can be tested. Institutional readiness is the record of how your institution actually operates — the exceptions, the handoffs, the workarounds the manual never captured.

Institutions that treat these as the same thing budget for the first and get surprised by the second. The platform runs. The institution runs the way it always did, now on more expensive software. Staff have learned the new interface. They are still following the old workflow underneath it.

This isn't a vendor problem. It isn't a project management problem. It's a paper problem. The paper was never there.

---

## Four questions to ask before the next cutover window closes.

**Can someone other than the originator run your highest-volume processes from what's on paper?**
The answer is almost always more uneven than cabinet expects. Aid offices document because Title IV makes it non-optional. The rest of the institution rarely matches that bar.

**When a cross-functional exception hits, can leadership name the decision authority in 60 seconds?**
Authority chains that fail this test are decoration. The actual routing is being reconstructed informally every time an exception lands — and the reconstruction depends on whoever is in the room that day.

**Would a federal reviewer find evidence the process was followed, or only evidence that it was declared?**
Evidence that lives in staff memory isn't evidence. It's intention. The reviewer tests the paper.

**When the system goes live, will the way your institution operates survive your registrar retiring?**
The best answer to this question is written down. The usual answer is "ask her." The usual answer doesn't survive.

---

## The paper is the readiness.

The institutions that exit cutover clean are the institutions that put the operating model on paper before configuration started. Not the policy. The procedure. The step-by-step of how work actually gets done, including the exceptions and the handoffs. The record survives the system change. The record survives the people who built it.

If your ERP went live tomorrow, how many of your critical cross-functional workflows would hold up when a reviewer asks "says who?"
